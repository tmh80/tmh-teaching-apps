How this model works (for teachers)
  • Sun position uses the standard noon-Sun rule (noon height = 90° − |latitude − Sun's overhead latitude|) and the day-length equation. Orbits are treated as circles, so there is no closer-or-further effect.
  • No atmospheric bending of light, so the equator gets exactly 12 hours of daylight and the pole exactly half a year of light. Real values are a little longer.
  • Earth temperatures come from a simple energy-balance model tuned to real Earth (Beijing about −5 °C in January, 28 °C in July). It has no land, oceans, ice or weather, so treat numbers for other tilts as trends, not forecasts. Real oceans would soften the extremes at big tilts.
  • Temperatures for other planets are published reference values, not modelled.
  • Moons move in simple circular orbits with correct sizes and speeds. Their positions are illustrative, not exact predictions. Gas giants have no surface; the sky view is from the cloud tops.
