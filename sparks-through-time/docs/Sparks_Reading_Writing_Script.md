# Sparks Through Time: reading and writing script

Quoted text is exact student text. *Teacher* items stay off student screens.

## Shared rules

**Teach first (Tom, Nov 2026):** every skill is taught with a worked example before students use it (`rwTeach`). Learn teaches the skill; the Check applies it in a new context and never repeats Learn.

**Routine:** a "Read it, Mark it, Write it" strip sits above each new step and paragraph, current word lit.

**Fading:** three starters in Ch 1, two in Ch 2, one in Ch 3, none in Ch 4. The reminder fold-out is open in Ch 1, closed in Ch 2 and 3, hidden in Ch 4.

**Key idea:** the largest text on its step.

**Mark it (`mark`):** tap a sentence to label it; each tap moves to the next label, then back to no label. Some sentences need no label. One go; one point per sentence. After Check, wrong sentences show the right label, and each shows its reason. Prints as numbered sentences with label boxes. Unscored in Learn.

**Paragraph card:** the `write` type with four ticks, titled "Data paragraph" or "Reading paragraph". Marked evidence sits beside the box, with corrected labels. In Ch 1 and 2 the student taps two rows or points first. Ticks below name a catalogue section 3 row.

**Check:** the listed items, Mark it, then the paragraph. Other questions stay in the data for retrieval and the Time Test.

**Strands** (*Teacher*, to confirm against WAB's guide): Cii "interpret data and outline results using scientific reasoning"; Ciii "discuss the validity of a prediction based on the outcome of the scientific investigation".

## Chapter 1: electromagnets

**Where:** three steps after "Plan a fair test". Adds approx. 10 min; shorter Check saves 8.

**Trim:** "Plan a fair test" finish becomes "Brilliant planning! Now let's run it."

### 1A Run your fair test

Rig: coils only, 2 cells, iron core, no wobble. Table on screen at once.

**say:** "You planned a fair test, and now you get to run it! Good readers read the question first. Tell me what you already know."  
**Question at top:** "Does the number of coils change how strong an electromagnet is?"  
**Key idea:** "Read the question first. Then you know what to look for."  
**Do this:** "Tap the two parts to look for. Type what you know. Then set the coils to 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50, and type the clips for each."  
**Answers:** "the number of coils", "how strong". Any typed text counts. 10→4, 20→8, 30→12, 40→16, 50→20 clips.  
**Hint:** "Look for what you change and what you measure."

### 1B Mark your evidence

**say:** "Scientists pick the numbers that show the pattern best. Which two rows would convince a friend?"  
**Key idea:** "Pick two numbers. Write each one with its unit, like 4 clips or 2.4 V."  
**Do this:** "Tap two rows, then answer." Any rows count. Feedback: "The first and last rows show the biggest change."  
**Q:** "Which sentence uses evidence best?" ✔ "With 10 coils it picked up 4 clips, and with 50 coils it picked up 20 clips."; "With 10 coils it picked up lots of clips, and with 50 coils it picked up more."; "With 10 and 50 coils it picked up 4 and 20, and that is a big jump."  
**Hint:** "Can a reader tell what each number means?"

### 1C Take apart a good paragraph

**say:** "Scientists share their results in a paragraph called a CER. Let's learn the three parts first. Then you will find them in a real paragraph."  
**Teach card, "CER: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning":** "A good science paragraph does three jobs, in this order." Example question: "Do dark shirts get hotter in the sun than light shirts?" Claim (answers the question): "Dark shirts get hotter in the sun than light shirts." Evidence (gives numbers from the test, with their units, like clips, kg or °C): "After 10 minutes the dark shirt was 38 °C, and the light shirt was 30 °C." Reasoning (explains why, using science): "Dark colours absorb more light energy, so the shirt heats up more." Button: "Got it. Let me try".  
**Do this:** "Here is a different test, with 1 to 4 cells and 30 coils each time. Mark each sentence as Claim, Evidence or Reasoning."  
**Labels:** Claim, Evidence, Reasoning.
  1. "The results show that more cells make a stronger electromagnet." Claim
  2. "With 1 cell it picked up 6 clips, and with 4 cells it picked up 24 clips." Evidence
  3. "More cells push a bigger current through the coil, so the magnetic field gets stronger." Reasoning
**Key idea (after marking):** "Claim, Evidence, Reasoning. Say it, show it, explain it."  
**Write:** "Your question was: does the number of coils change how strong an electromagnet is? Look at your results. Write one claim that answers this question." A graph of the coil results sits beside the box, headed "My results". Any text counts. Model: "My results show that more coils make a stronger electromagnet."  
**Hint:** "Try starting with: My results show that…"

### Check

Keep Q1 table (Aii), Q2 num (Aiii), Q3 multi (Ai), Q6 sort (Di).

**Mark it** (*Teacher*: Cii). "Mia tested different cores, with 30 coils and 2 cells each time. Mark each sentence. Leave it with no label if it doesn't belong." Labels: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning.

1. "The results show that an iron core makes the strongest electromagnet." Claim: "It answers the question."
2. "With an iron core it picked up 12 clips." Evidence: "A number with a unit."
3. "I enjoyed using the iron nail the most." No label: "A feeling is no help here."
4. "With an aluminium core it picked up 1 clip." Evidence: "A second number to compare."
5. "Iron becomes a strong magnet inside the coil, so the magnetic field is much stronger." Reasoning: "It explains why."

**Data paragraph** (*Teacher*: Cii).

**Prompt:** "A scrapyard crane uses an electromagnet to lift old cars and scrap steel. The workers kept the coils the same and changed the number of cells. Their question: does the number of cells change how strong the crane's electromagnet is? Tap two points on the graph, then answer the question."  
**Graph:** 1 cell 500 kg, 2 cells 1,000 kg, 3 cells 1,500 kg, 4 cells 2,000 kg. Prints on the worksheet.  
**Words to use:** cells, mass, magnetic field, stronger  
**Starters:** "The results show that…"; "With … cells it lifted…"; "This happens because…"  
**Model (numbers from the points tapped):** "The results show that more cells make the crane's electromagnet stronger. With 1 cell it lifted a mass of 500 kg, and with 4 cells it lifted 2,000 kg. This happens because more cells push a bigger current through the coils, so the magnetic field gets stronger and holds more steel."  
**Ticks:** data "what do the results show?" row; science tick "I explained the pattern using the magnetic field."

## Chapter 2: generators

**Where:** three steps after "More power!". Adds approx. 10 min; trim and shorter Check save 9.

**Trim:** delete "Read the wave"; "Spin it!" shows the wave and "Hamster power" teaches AC. New "More power!" say: "Spinning faster is only one way. Can you get the peak voltage up to at least 12 V? Try changing the coil and the magnet too."

### 2A Crack a tricky word

Rig: turns of wire only, 60%, weak magnet. Empty graph beside it: "Turns of wire" 0 to 500, "Peak voltage (V)" 0 to 7.

**say:** "Graphs have tricky words too. Look at this label: peak voltage. A mountain has a peak. Have a guess before you read on!"  
**Key idea:** "Stuck on a word? Use the clues around it: the picture, the label and the sentence."  
**Do this:** "Answer the word questions. Then set the turns of wire to 100, 200, 300, 400 and 500. Type each peak voltage and tap Plot."  
**Q1:** "What does peak voltage mean?" ✔ "The highest voltage the wave reaches"; "The lowest voltage the wave reaches"; "The middle voltage of the wave"  
**Q2:** "What does turns of wire mean on this graph?" ✔ "The loops of wire in the coil"; "The times the magnet goes round"; "The bends in the wire to the bulb"  
**Hint (both):** "Look at the green screen and the picture of the coil."  
**Points:** 100→1.2, 200→2.4, 300→3.6, 400→4.8, 500→6.0 V.

### 2B Test the hypothesis

**say:** "Here is a hypothesis: twice the turns of wire gives twice the peak voltage. Tell me what you already know. Which two points can test it?"  
**Key idea:** "Test the exact words of the hypothesis."  
**Do this:** "Type what you know. Tap two points where the turns of wire double." Accept 100 and 200, or 200 and 400.  
**Hint:** "Find a point, then find the one with twice as many turns."  
**Q1:** "How many times bigger is the peak voltage at your second point?" Answer 2 (2.4 ÷ 1.2, or 4.8 ÷ 2.4).  

### 2C Weak or strong?

**say:** "Let's learn how to write about a hypothesis. Then you will judge two students' paragraphs."  
**Teach card, "A strong hypothesis paragraph":** "It works like a CER, but the claim says if the data support or contradict the hypothesis." Example hypothesis: "Dark shirts get hotter in the sun than light shirts." Say support or contradict: "The data support this hypothesis." Show two numbers: "After 10 minutes the dark shirt was 38 °C, and the light shirt was 30 °C." Explain the science: "Dark colours absorb more light energy, so the shirt heats up more."  
**A:** "The data contradict the hypothesis. The line on the graph goes up. It was a really good generator."  
**B:** "The data contradict the hypothesis. At 2 turns each second the peak was 1.0 V, but at 10 turns each second it was 5.0 V. The magnet moves past the coil faster, so it makes a bigger voltage."  
**Do this:** "Two students tested this hypothesis: faster spinning makes the peak voltage smaller. Tap the stronger paragraph. Then pick the three cards that make it strong."  
**Answer:** B. ✔ "It says support or contradict"; ✔ "It uses two numbers with units"; ✔ "It explains the science"; "It uses very long words"; "It tells us what the student liked"  
**Hint:** "What does B add?"  
**Key idea (the student's cards):** "Say support or contradict. Show two numbers. Explain the science." These fill the Ch 2 fold-out.

### Check

Keep Q2 order (Di), Q3 multi (Ai), Q5 mcq (Aii), Q6 num (Aii).

Q6 fix: "in step with the turns each second" and "at 7 turns each second". Answer stays 5.6 V.

**Mark it** (*Teacher*: Cii). "Sam tested this hypothesis: a stronger magnet gives a bigger peak voltage. Mark each sentence. The claim could be anywhere!" Labels: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning.

1. "With a weak magnet the peak voltage was 1.2 V." Evidence: "A number with a unit."
2. "With a strong magnet it was 3.6 V." Evidence: "A second number to compare."
3. "We used a red generator." No label: "True, but colour doesn't test the hypothesis."
4. "A stronger magnet makes a bigger voltage in the coil as it moves past." Reasoning: "It explains why."
5. "So the data support the hypothesis." Claim: "It says support or contradict."

**Data paragraph** (*Teacher*: Cii, Ciii).

**Prompt:** "A bike dynamo is a small generator. The wheel spins a magnet next to a coil of wire. A cyclist says: 'If my wheel turns twice as fast, the dynamo makes twice the peak voltage.' Tap two points on the graph that test this. Do the data support or contradict it?"  
**Graph:** 2 turns each second 1.6 V, 4 turns 3.2 V, 6 turns 4.8 V, 8 turns 6.4 V. Prints on the worksheet.  
**Words to use:** turns each second, moving magnet, peak voltage, twice  
**Starters:** "The data…"; "At … turns each second the peak voltage was…"  
**Model:** "The data support this hypothesis. At 2 turns each second the peak voltage was 1.6 V, and at 4 turns each second it was 3.2 V. That is twice as fast and twice the peak voltage. The moving magnet passes the coil faster, so it makes a bigger voltage."  
**Ticks:** data "support or contradict?" row; science tick "I explained the pattern using a magnet moving past the coil faster."

## Chapter 3: acdc

**Where:** three steps replace "Who won?"; its story moves to the Check passage and Q6 tests its idea. Adds approx. 9 min; this swap and shorter Check save 8.

### 3A Spot the structure

**say:** "Science texts often have three parts. Let's learn them first. Then you will find them in a text about your phone charger."  
**Teach card, "Problem, Science, Solution":** "Many science texts do three jobs. Find the jobs and the text gets easier to read." Example text: keeping a hospital running. Problem (what is going wrong?): "A storm can cut the power to a hospital." Science (how does it work?): "A backup generator spins a magnet inside coils of wire, and this makes electricity." Solution (what is better now?): "The lights and machines keep running until the power comes back."  
**Do this:** "Mark each sentence as Problem, Science or Solution."  
**Labels:** Problem, Science, Solution.
  1. "A phone battery needs about 5 V of DC, but a socket in Beijing gives 220 V of AC." Problem
  2. "A transformer inside the charger lowers the voltage." Science
  3. "Another part changes the AC to DC." Science
  4. "Now your phone can charge safely from the wall." Solution
**Key idea (after marking):** "Problem, science, solution. Find all three."  
**Hint:** "What would go wrong without the charger?"

### 3B Put the cards in order

**say:** "A paragraph needs its sentences in the right order. Put these mixed-up cards in the order a reader needs."  
**Do this:** "Move the cards."  
**Cards:** the four 3A sentences, shuffled.  
**Hint:** "Start with the problem and end with the solution."

### 3C Fix a weak paragraph

**say:** "This paragraph is about the same charger, but we learn very little from it. Can you fix it?"  
**Weak:** "Chargers are really useful. A charger changes the electricity. My phone works."  
**Key idea:** "Swap vague words for exact ones."  
**Do this:** "Tick what is missing. Then rewrite it in your own words."  
**Q:** ✔ "The problem"; ✔ "The science idea"; ✔ "A number from the text"; "A title"; "A joke"  
**Hint:** "'Changes the electricity' is vague. What exactly changes?"  
**Rewrite:** any text counts. Model: the 3A text.

### Check

Keep Q2 num (Aii), Q4 sort (Ai), Q5 order (Di), Q6 mcq (Di).

**Mark it, "The Waterfall and the City"** (*Teacher*: Di). "Read the question first: how did engineers get electricity from Niagara Falls to Buffalo? Mark each sentence. Leave it with no label if it doesn't help." Labels: Problem, Science, Solution. Glossary pop-up, epoch: "a long period of time".

1. "Niagara Falls could make enough electricity for a whole city, but Buffalo was about 40 km away." Problem: "The power was far from the city."
2. "Edison's low-voltage DC wasted so much energy as heat that customers had to live less than 2 km from a power station." Problem: "It shows what stopped DC."
3. "In 1889 he wrote that he had 'always consistently opposed' high-voltage AC." No label: "This is his view."
4. "A transformer can raise the voltage of AC." Science: "How the machine works."
5. "When the voltage goes up, the current goes down, so the wires waste far less energy." Science: "Why less energy is wasted."
6. "In November 1896, engineers used transformers to send AC from the falls to Buffalo at 11,000 V." Solution: "What they did."
7. "Buffalo's electric trams could now run on power from the falls." Solution: "What was better afterwards."
8. "Nikola Tesla called this 'a new and glorious epoch in the history of humanity.'" No label: "This is praise."

After marking, keep the "Who won?" DC note, with 1896.

**Reading paragraph** (*Teacher*: Di, Dii, Diii).

**Prompt:** "Edison's DC could not reach Buffalo from Niagara Falls. Explain how engineers solved this problem."  
**Words to use:** transformer, high voltage, current, heat  
**Starter:** "The problem was that…"  
**Model:** "The problem was that Buffalo was about 40 km from Niagara Falls, and long wires turned most of the energy in low-voltage DC into heat. AC solved this because a transformer can raise its voltage. At high voltage the current is smaller, so the wires waste far less energy."  
**Ticks:** reading "explain how" row.

## Chapter 4: power

**Where:** two steps after "Compare the CO₂". Adds approx. 7 min; shorter Check saves 7, so no Learn trim.

### 4A Fact or opinion?

**say:** "Some sentences can be checked, and some tell you what a person thinks. Let's learn the rule first. Then you will sort some sentences."  
**Teach card, "Fact or opinion?":** "Good readers check which sentences they can trust." Fact (can be checked with a measurement, a chart or a record): "Water boils at 100 °C at sea level." Opinion (tells you what someone thinks; words like should, best and ugly are clues): "Everyone should drink more tea."  
**Do this:** "Sort each sentence. Then write one fact and one opinion about coal."  
**Fact:** "Coal releases about 820 g of CO₂ for each kWh."; "A hydro dam needs a river."  
**Opinion:** "Solar panels look ugly on old roofs."; "Every town should ban coal."  
**Hint:** "Could you check it with the chart or a measurement?"  
**Key idea (after sorting):** "A fact can be checked. An opinion is what someone thinks."  
**Typed job:** any text counts. Model: "Fact: coal is a fossil fuel. Opinion: coal is the worst source."

### 4B Sum it up

**say:** "Good readers see both sides. Let's learn how to sum up both sides. Then you will try it with solar panels."  
**Teach card, "Sum up both sides":** "A good summary gives a benefit and a drawback in one sentence." Example question: should our school buy electric buses? Benefit: "Electric buses release no fumes on the road." Drawback: "They need hours to charge." Sum it up (join both sides with the word but): "Electric buses release no fumes on the road, but they need hours to charge."  
**Do this:** "Read the question first: should our school put solar panels on its roof? Mark each sentence. Then sum it up in one sentence."  
**Labels:** Benefit, Drawback.
  1. "For each kWh, solar panels release about 48 g of CO₂, and coal releases about 820 g." Benefit
  2. "Sunlight is free, and it will not run out." Benefit
  3. "Solar panels make no electricity at night." Drawback
**Key idea:** "Sum it up: one sentence, both sides."  
**Hint:** "Try putting the word but in the middle."  
**Typed job:** any text counts. Model: "Solar panels release far less CO₂ than coal, but they make no electricity at night."

### Check

Keep Q1 order (Di), Q3 num (Aii), Q5 match (Aiii), Q6 mcq (Ai).

**Mark it, "Is It Time for Wind?"** (*Teacher*: Dii). "Read the question first: should a wind farm replace a coal power station? Mark each sentence. Even famous scientists give opinions!" Labels: Benefit of wind, Drawback of wind, Opinion.

1. "In 1881, the scientist William Thomson thought that when coal ran low, 'windmills or wind-motors' would take over." Opinion: "Nobody could check this in 1881."
2. "For each kWh, coal releases about 820 g of CO₂, and a wind turbine releases about 11 g, counting its whole life." Benefit: "Far less CO₂, and you can check it."
3. "On a still day, a wind turbine makes no electricity." Drawback: "No wind means no power."
4. "Nobody has to mine the wind or pay for it." Benefit: "The energy source is free."
5. "Wind turbines look beautiful on a green hill." Opinion: "People can disagree."
6. "A coal power station can run at any time, so a town with only wind needs a backup." Drawback: "Wind can't always meet the need."

**Reading paragraph** (*Teacher*: Di, Dii, Diii).

**Prompt:** "Your town's old coal power station is closing. Should a wind farm take its place? Give one benefit, one drawback and your opinion with a reason."  
**Words to use:** renewable, CO₂, reliable, backup  
**Model:** "One benefit is that wind is renewable, and a wind turbine releases only about 11 g of CO₂ for each kWh, while coal releases about 820 g. One drawback is that wind is less reliable, because a still day means no electricity. I think the town should build the wind farm and keep a backup for still days, because less CO₂ means less warming of the planet."  
**Ticks:** reading "benefit and drawback" row.

## Every chapter (added 6 Nov 2026)

**Read first:** in every Learn step, everything below Pip's message stays blurred until the student presses OK. Remembered once pressed.

**I can screen:** Learn opens on its own first screen of I can statements. Students press Next to reach step 1, and Back on step 1 returns to it.

**Self-check:** immediately before the Foundation review, students place an X on a Beginning, Developing, Proficient, Mastering line for each I can statement. The questions appear after that. On screen only, not on worksheets.

**Answer order:** table, match and sort rows show in a stable shuffled order (`gridRowOrder`), and order questions never start correct. `keepRows` keeps truth tables and other true sequences in order.

**I can statements:**
- **launch:** I can spot things that use electricity in everyday life. I can describe how charged objects push and pull on each other. I can explain the difference between static electricity and current electricity.
- **atom:** I can describe how the model of the atom changed over time. I can name the protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom and say where they are. I can use the atomic number and mass number to build an atom.
- **static:** I can explain how rubbing moves electrons from one object to another. I can work out if an object has a positive or negative charge. I can predict if two charged objects will repel or attract. I can explain how lightning happens and how a lightning rod protects a house.
- **circuits:** I can name the parts of a circuit and match them to their symbols. I can build a complete circuit with a switch. I can explain why a shortcut in a circuit stops a bulb from lighting. I can use an ammeter to measure current.
- **electromagnets:** I can explain how an electric current makes a magnetic field. I can describe how to make an electromagnet stronger. I can run a fair test and pick the best evidence from my results. I can write a CER paragraph with a claim, evidence and reasoning.
- **ohm:** I can sort materials into conductors and insulators. I can explain why metals conduct electricity. I can describe what a resistor does to the current. I can use V = I × R to work out voltage, current or resistance.
- **generators:** I can explain how moving a magnet near a coil of wire makes electricity. I can describe how to make a generator give a bigger voltage. I can test a hypothesis using data. I can write a paragraph that says if data support or contradict a hypothesis.
- **bulb:** I can name the parts of a light bulb and say what they do. I can explain why a filament glows. I can explain what gets used up when a bulb is lit. I can compare a filament bulb with an LED.
- **series:** I can describe the difference between series and parallel circuits. I can predict what happens to the other bulbs when one bulb is taken out. I can work out the current in series and parallel circuits.
- **acdc:** I can describe the difference between AC and DC. I can explain how high voltage helps send electricity to a town. I can find the problem, the science and the solution in a science text. I can write a clear paragraph about how science solves a problem.
- **power:** I can explain how a spinning turbine helps a power station make electricity. I can compare the CO₂ from different energy sources. I can tell a fact from an opinion. I can sum up the benefits and drawbacks of an energy source.
- **future:** I can sort energy sources into renewable and non-renewable. I can explain how solar panels make electricity without a turbine. I can plan how to keep a town’s lights on using different energy sources.
