For teachers: about the model
  • Gravitational field strength is g = 9.8 N/kg throughout.
  • Force Arenas pullers give a steady force: kid 60 N, dog 70 N, adult 130 N, strong adult 200 N, pony 260 N, horse 650 N, husky 90 N, strongman 1250 N, elephant 4600 N. The numbers are deliberately uneven so that teams can be balanced in more than one way. Real pullers are less steady.
  • Tug-of-war floors: air floor μ = 0 (an air cushion, like an air-hockey table), ice 0.03, smooth 0.1, rough concrete 0.4, with one coefficient for sticking and sliding. Maximum friction = coefficient × weight. Below that, friction matches the pull and the object stays still.
  • Husky Sled Race: sled 30 kg plus a 70 kg musher plus cargo. Packed snow μ = 0.07 to start and 0.05 sliding, fresh powder 0.18 and 0.15. The huskies' pull tapers off near their 9 m/s top speed, so the forces balance there. The dogs' own mass is left out.
  • Strongman Pull: separate static and sliding coefficients, so friction drops once the load moves. Wheels on tarmac 0.015 / 0.01 and on sand 0.12 / 0.1; locked wheels (handbrake) 0.8 / 0.7 and 0.6 / 0.5; the boulder sledge 0.5 / 0.35 and 0.65 / 0.5.
  • Space Dome: modelled on NASA's precision air-bearing floor, so weight and the normal force balance and there is no friction, which matches sideways motion in space. Air resistance is ignored at these low speeds. Each push lasts exactly 1 second (smoothed at the ends), so the pod leaves at F ÷ m and the astronaut (60–200 kg, set by tool packs) recoils at F ÷ her mass. Speeds are logged just after each push ends, and predictions count as correct within 0.01 m/s or 2%. Mission control orders pick a pod mass and a speed window that one of the push settings will hit. Thruster bursts are 20 N for 1 second. Docking counts at 0.5 m/s or slower.
  • The rope is treated as weightless, so the crate gets the full pull of each team.
  • Crash Bay: each collision uses a constant force for a set time (rigid fronts 40 ms, crumple zones 150 ms), so force = mass × change in speed ÷ time. Real crash forces rise and fall, so these are average forces. The safety test uses a 70 kg dummy, 1200 kg car, and simplified stopping times for each safety feature. Injury risk bands are for illustration only.
  • Machine challenges: each problem has a rope or space limit, so the least force possible is the load ÷ the largest mechanical advantage that fits that limit. The app works out the best answer for every problem and awards stars against it.
  • Machines Workshop: machines are ideal, with no friction and weightless beams, ropes and pulleys, so work in always equals work out. The lever rule (effort × effort distance = load × load distance) is shown as an extension. The winch drum radius is fixed at 0.1 m.
  • Skid Track: one friction coefficient per surface (perfect ice 0, ice 0.05, polished wood 0.2, carpet 0.4, sandpaper 0.8), used for both sticking and sliding. Stopping distance = v² ÷ (2 × coefficient × g). Air resistance is ignored.
