# Force HQ: graphs, CER and evaluating (plan, updated 7 Nov 2026)

All of Tom's decisions are in and the plan is signed off (6 Nov 2026). On 7 Nov it was brought into line with everything added to Sparks since: graph making and graph reading as taught skills, scatter or bar, I can statements, the self-check and larger text. Nothing has been built. It replaces Backlog job 3, the CER review. The Check contexts are agreed. Example data and real-world facts get checked at the script stage.

## 1. What students will learn

1. **Make graphs:** choose scatter or bar, write the title and axis labels with units, set the scale and plot the points.
2. **Read and analyse graphs:** read a value, describe the trend with numbers, pick the best evidence, test a hypothesis and tell the story of a velocity–time graph.
3. **Write CER paragraphs:** first from their own data, then with less support.
4. **Evaluate an application of science:** use society, environment, economy and health, with SDG targets where they fit, to reach a judgement.

Every section opens with its I can statements, and students rate themselves against them just before its Check (section 10, parts 11 and 12).

One frame runs through all three, so students only learn it once:

| Task | Claim | Evidence | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data paragraph | What the data show | Two numbers with units | The science that explains the pattern |
| Hypothesis paragraph | How far the data support the hypothesis | Numbers that test it | The science that explains why |
| Judgement | My choice | Pros and cons, with facts | Which factor matters most here, and why |

Written so they can be lifted into the skill (section 11). The Sparks comment or event behind each rule is in brackets.

## 3. What is different about Force HQ

- **Sections can be set alone.** A later section cannot assume CER was met earlier, so each teach card must work on its own.
- **Audience.** Grade 7/8 voice: enthusiastic, natural full sentences. Students say "velocity". Grades 7 and 8 get the same support (Tom, 6 Nov).
- **Graphs and data.** Velocity–time graphs already appear in Chapters 2, 3 and 5, and the app keeps a history of each run. Students read these graphs but are never taught how, and they do not yet plot two variables of their own.
- **Strands.** Force HQ has no Objective C tags yet, and its only Check question aimed at D ii is in the closed Workshop (`c6r26`).
- **Unit timing.** The forces unit assesses B/C through the helicopter drop (lessons 6 to 9), with Criterion A at the end. Graph and CER work before lesson 9 will help most. New data must stay clear of the helicopter investigation.
- **Saves are easier.** Check answers are stored by question id, so new ids cannot collide with old answers.

## 5. MYP Year 3 wording and command terms

These go on the teacher page, the AI marking text and the question tags. Students see the command terms as plain verbs in questions, with a glossary pop-up, and no strand codes.

| Strand | Year 3 wording | What it asks for here |
|---|---|---|
| C i | present collected and transformed data | Choose the graph type, label it, set the scale and plot accurately |
| C ii | interpret data and describe results using scientific reasoning | Find the trend, give numbers, use science to say why |
| C iii | discuss the validity of a hypothesis based on the outcome of the scientific investigation | Weigh how well all the data fit the hypothesis, then conclude |
| D i | describe the ways in which science is applied and used to address a specific problem or issue | A detailed account of how the science helps |
| D ii | discuss and analyse the various implications of using science and its application in solving a specific problem or issue | Break the issue into factors, review both sides, conclude with evidence |
| D iii | apply scientific language effectively | Use the right terms and units |

| Command term | Meaning | Student-friendly pop-up (draft) |
|---|---|---|
| Interpret | Use knowledge to recognise trends and draw conclusions | Find the pattern and say what it means |
| Describe | Give a detailed account or picture | Say what happens, with details |
| Analyse | Break down to bring out the essential elements or structure | Break it into parts and see how they connect |
| Discuss | A considered, balanced review with a range of arguments, supported by evidence | Look at different sides, use evidence, then decide |

Two consequences for marking:
- "Discuss" means a hypothesis paragraph uses more than one comparison from the data, and says how far the data support it.
- "Discuss and analyse" means a judgement covers at least two factors and both sides, then reaches a conclusion.

## 6. The graph thread

Graph skills are taught once, then practised with less help. Each one gets a teach card with a worked example before students use it.

| Section | Make | Read | Teach card |
|---|---|---|---|
| `c2s` | Scatter graph of their own crate data, with live help | Read a value; describe the trend with two numbers | "How to make a good graph" (worked example from forces but not tied to one concept, such as how far a spring stretches with different masses hung on it), then "Scatter or bar?" with three practice questions |
| `c3s` | Scatter graph in the Check, no help | Use the exact words a hypothesis makes, such as "twice" | Reminder only |
| `c4s` | None | None | None |
| `c5s` | Bar graph in the Check (for example, how long each aid option takes to arrive) | Tell the story of a velocity–time graph, stage by stage | "Reading a velocity–time graph": flat means steady, up means speeding up, down means slowing down |
| `c6s` | Curved scatter graph of their own ramp data | Say what happens to one variable as the other goes up | Reminder only |

**Scatter or bar.** If you changed a number, use a scatter graph. If you changed a name or a group, use a bar graph. The three practice questions use forces contexts, such as different surfaces (bar), different masses (scatter) and different shoes (bar).

**Graph Check question.** Worth 5 marks, one each for the graph type, title, both axis labels, sensible axes and accurate points. Students get one go. It prints on the student worksheet, the blank sheet has the table and an empty grid, and the answer key shows a correct graph. Strand C i.

**Graph-reading Check questions.** Short questions with one right answer, such as a value read off the graph or the stage where the forces are balanced. These mark C ii alongside the paragraph.

**Existing velocity–time graphs.** The skills audit (section 10) decides whether Chapters 2, 3 and 5 also need the reading card before their own v–t questions.

## 8. Section by section

All contexts below are agreed.

**`c2s` Graphs and CER**
- **Learn 1:** brake crates of 20, 40, 60 and 80 kg with the 36 N brake at 2.4 m/s, type each distance, then learn how to make a good graph and choose scatter or bar, and make the graph themselves. Expected: 1.6, 3.2, 4.8 and 6.4 m. Read a value off it, describe the pattern and pick two points.
- **Learn 2:** CER teach card, with a worked example away from crates. Mark a paragraph, then write a claim about their own test with its question and graph beside the box.
- **Check:** make a graph of a loaded truck's results on a test track, load against stopping distance; then Mark it; then a CER paragraph that uses the same data as a table. Hook: SDG 3.6, road safety.

**`c3s` Test a hypothesis**
- **Learn 1:** 1 to 4 huskies pull a 60 kg sled with no friction. Expected: 1.5, 3.0, 4.5 and 6.0 m/s². Make the graph, then test "twice the pull gives twice the acceleration".
- **Learn 2:** teach card on how a hypothesis paragraph differs from a CER, then judge a weak and a strong paragraph.
- **Check:** make a graph of the tugboat and container ship data; then a hypothesis paragraph, using the same data as a table, on a claim the data contradict, such as "twice the tugboats gives four times the acceleration".

**`c4s` Science solving a problem**
- **Learn 1:** Problem, Science, Solution teach card, with a short worked example.
- **Learn 2:** mark a text about astronauts moving outside a spacecraft, then fix a weak paragraph.
- **Check context:** "Rockets that land", how reusable rockets use force pairs to slow down. Hook: SDG 12.5, reducing waste through reuse.

**`c5s` Weigh it up**
- **Learn 1:** "Reading a velocity–time graph" teach card, then tell the story of a skydive graph stage by stage, saying where the forces are balanced.
- **Learn 2:** factors teach card, with an everyday worked example and one SDG target. Sort facts under society, environment, economy and health, for or against, then say which matters most.
- **Check:** a flood cuts off a town, so should aid arrive by parachute drop or by another route? Students make a bar graph of how long each option takes, read the velocity–time graph of a crate landing, then write a judgement. Hook: SDG 11.5, disasters. This replaces the Mars landing idea, which has no clear SDG link.
- **Timing:** this section has the most in it, so `secMins` decides whether the graph reading moves to Learn only.

**`c6s` Ramp or lift?**
- **Learn 1:** pull the 490 N cart up ramps of different lengths to a 1 m platform and make a graph of the effort, then describe what happens to the effort as the ramp gets longer. It should fall from 245 N at 2 m to 98 N at 5 m on the Perfect setting.
- **Learn 2:** a short reminder of factors, then a worked judgement to mark.
- **Check context:** should a school fit a ramp or a lift for wheelchair access? Students make their own judgement with very little support. Hook: SDG 4.a, disability-sensitive education facilities.
- **Timing:** this waits for the rescue rebuild, and `c6r26` stays as it is.

## 9. Factors for evaluating

Students use **society, environment, economy and health**. Safety sits under health. Where relevant, a question names one **SDG target** in plain words, and students judge the options against it. Only use a target with a real link to the context.

A judgement says: "I would choose…", gives facts on both sides from at least two factors, then "The most important factor here is… because…". Any reasoned choice can earn full marks, so the ticks reward the reasoning.

**Candidate targets** (exact wording to be confirmed at the script stage):
- 3.6: halve deaths and injuries from road traffic accidents.
- 4.a: build and upgrade education facilities that are child, disability and gender sensitive.
- 11.2: safe, affordable, accessible and sustainable transport for all.
- 11.5: reduce the deaths, people affected and economic losses caused by disasters.
- 12.5: substantially reduce waste through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse.
