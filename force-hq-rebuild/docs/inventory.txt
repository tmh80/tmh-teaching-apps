== Learn 5 (s3r): The overall force
  CARD: When two teams pull against each other, their forces partly cancel out. What is left over is the resultant force: the overall force on the crate.Forces in opposite directions: take the smaller one away from the bigger one. The resultant points the same way as the bigger force. Forces in the same direction: add them together.Left team 130 N, right team 200 N: the resultant is 200 − 130 = 70 N to the right.
  CARD: If both teams pull equally hard, the resultant is 0 N. The forces are balanced and the crate doesn't start to move.If one team pulls harder, the forces are unbalanced, so there is a resultant force and the crate starts to move. Today the crate sits on a special floor with no friction, so you can see this clearly. You'll meet friction in the next step.
  TASK: Make the forces balanced (the same total pull on each side), then press Pull! and watch.

== Learn 9 (s7): Contact and non-contact forces
  CARD: Some forces only act when things touch. These are contact forces: a push, the Tension in a rope, friction and the normal force.
  CARD: Other forces act across a gap, without touching. These are non-contact forces. Gravity is one: the Earth pulls on you even when you jump. Magnetic force is another: a magnet can pull a paper clip from a distance.
  CARD: One more contact force to meet: air resistance. When something moves through the air, the air pushes back against it. You will test it properly in the Drop Zone in Chapter 5.
  TASK: Press Pull! and watch the ropes go tight. The pull in a tight rope is tension, a contact force.

  CARD: The heavy crate was much harder to get moving. Scientists say it has more Inertia.Inertia is how hard it is to change the way an object is moving.
