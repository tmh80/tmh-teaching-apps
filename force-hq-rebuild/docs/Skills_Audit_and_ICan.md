## 3. I can statements

Shown on each section's own first screen and behind the "My I can statements" button; students rate them in the self-check just before the Check. New Skills sections are marked ★.

**`c1f` What is a force?**
- I can describe a force as a push or a pull measured in newtons.
- I can work out the resultant force and say whether forces are balanced or unbalanced.
- I can calculate an object's weight using weight = mass × 9.8.
- I can sort forces into contact and non-contact forces.

**`c1x` Free-body diagrams**
- I can draw a free-body diagram with every arrow starting at the centre of the object.
- I can use a scale to draw each force arrow the right length.
- I can use a free-body diagram to work out whether an object will start to move.

**`c2f` Inertia**
- I can explain why objects with more mass are harder to start moving and harder to stop.
- I can read a velocity from a velocity–time graph.
- I can state Newton's first law and use it to explain everyday examples.

**★ `c2s` Graphs and CER**
- I can choose a scatter graph or a bar graph for my data.
- I can make a graph with a title, labelled axes and accurate points.
- I can read a value from a graph and describe the trend using numbers.
- I can write a CER paragraph with a claim, evidence and reasoning.

**`c2x` Inertia Extension**
- I can describe how an object is moving from the shape of a velocity–time graph.
- I can explain why a heavier vehicle needs a bigger brake force to stop in the same distance.
- I can draw the free-body diagram of an object gliding at a steady velocity.

**`c3f` F = ma**
- I can describe how the resultant force and the mass change an object's acceleration.
- I can use F = m × a and the formula triangle to find the force, the mass or the acceleration.
- I can work out the resultant force when friction acts, then use it in F = m × a.

**★ `c3s` Test a hypothesis**
- I can make a graph of my results.
- I can test a hypothesis using numbers from the data.
- I can write a paragraph that says how far the data support or contradict a hypothesis.

**`c3x` F = ma Extension**
- I can find an acceleration from the slope of a velocity–time graph.
- I can solve two-step problems: the resultant force first, then F = m × a.
- I can turn a weight into a mass before I use F = m × a.

**`c4f` Action and reaction**
- I can state Newton's third law and describe a force pair.
- I can explain why the two forces in a pair do not cancel each other out.
- I can use F = m × a to explain why the lighter object in a pair moves faster.

**★ `c4s` Science solving a problem**
- I can find the problem, the science and the solution in a science text.
- I can describe how science is used to solve a problem.
- I can use scientific words correctly in my writing.

**`c4x` Action and reaction Extension**
- I can explain how thrusters and rockets use force pairs.
- I can work out the push needed to reach a target velocity.
- I can choose a mass that keeps a velocity under a safe limit.

**`c5f` Falling and terminal velocity**
- I can describe how weight and air resistance change during a fall.
- I can use balanced forces to explain terminal velocity.
- I can explain what happens to the forces and the velocity when a parachute opens.

**★ `c5s` Weigh it up**
- I can make a bar graph.
- I can describe what happens at each stage of a velocity–time graph.
- I can sort facts under society, environment, economy and health.
- I can make a judgement and explain which factor matters most.

**`c5x` Falling Extension**
- I can explain why heavy and light objects fall differently in air, but not in a vacuum.
- I can use weight = mass × g to find an object's weight on different worlds.
- I can explain how a hovering helicopter's lift balances its weight.
- I can choose a canopy and an opening height for a safe landing.

**`c6a` Levers**
- I can name the fulcrum, the load and the effort on a lever.
- I can explain how moving the fulcrum changes the effort.
- I can describe the catch: a smaller effort means a bigger distance.
- I can name the three classes of lever.

**`c6b` Pulleys, wheels and ramps**
- I can describe how pulleys, wheels and axles, ramps, wedges and screws help a small effort move a big load.
- I can say how more ropes, a longer handle or a longer ramp changes the effort.
- I can explain why a smaller effort needs a bigger distance.

**`c6c` Mechanical advantage and work**
- I can work out mechanical advantage as load ÷ effort.
- I can find mechanical advantage from a machine's shape and use it to predict the effort.
- I can calculate work in joules using work = force × distance.
- I can explain why a perfect machine does the same work, however much it reduces the effort.

**`c6d` Compound machines**
- I can describe a compound machine as simple machines working together.
- I can multiply mechanical advantages to find the total.
- I can work out the effort and the hand travel for a compound machine.

**`c6e` Rescue Captain Smooshy!**
- I can design a route of machines to rescue a spaceship.
- I can predict the effort before I test my machine.
- I can explain why a bigger mechanical advantage never reduces the work.
- I can weigh up two plans and choose one with reasons.

**★ `c6s` Ramp or lift?**
- I can make a graph of my ramp results and describe its curved trend.
- I can weigh up the pros and cons of a solution using different factors.
- I can write a judgement that says which factor matters most.

**`c6f` Real machines waste energy**
- I can explain how friction wastes energy in a machine.
- I can calculate the efficiency of a machine.
- I can choose a machine that is strong enough and wastes little energy.
