HOW TO MARK
- Student answers are printed in blue. Mark only what the student added. The worksheet's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
- Students don't need to match the answer key word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
- The feedback page at the end of each PDF shows how the student did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the visible answer and say so.
- The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
- Auto-marked questions in the sim allowed one go each, so mark the answer exactly as printed.
- A blank question scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
- The top of the worksheet shows "My self-check before the Check". It is the student's own self-review: for each I can statement they placed an X on a line from Beginning to Developing, Proficient and Mastering. It is not marked and earns no marks, so do not grade or correct it. In the feedback you may add at most one short sentence comparing how sure they felt with how they did.
- Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
- Mark each worksheet separately. An optional worksheet that wasn't submitted is not counted.

Q5 Tick all that apply, 2 marks [A i]: The two forces in a pair are the same size; The two forces in a pair act on different objects; The two forces in a pair point in opposite directions. 2 marks for exactly these; 1 mark if one is missing or one extra is ticked; otherwise 0.
Q6 Choose one, 1 mark [A ii]: The lighter skater goes twice as fast.
Q7 Match it up, 4 marks, 1 per row [A ii]: A hand pushes the table down → The table pushes the hand up; A boot kicks a ball forwards → The ball pushes the boot backwards; A rope pulls a crate to the right → The crate pulls the rope to the left; A swimmer pushes the water backwards → The water pushes the swimmer forwards.
Q8 Explain it, 3 marks, 1 per point [A ii]: The question gives the words to use (force, third law, opposite); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says the astronaut pushes the tool, and the tool pushes back on the astronaut • Says the two forces are the same size and in opposite directions (Newton's third law) • Says the force on the astronaut makes them move the opposite way to the tool Model answer: The astronaut pushes the tool with a force, so the tool pushes back on the astronaut with a force of the same size in the opposite direction (Newton's third law). This force makes the astronaut accelerate in the opposite direction to the tool, so the astronaut drifts away.
