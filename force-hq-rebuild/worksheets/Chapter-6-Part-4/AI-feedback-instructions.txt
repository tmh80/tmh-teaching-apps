HOW TO MARK
- Student answers are printed in blue. Mark only what the student added. The worksheet's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
- Students don't need to match the answer key word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
- The feedback page at the end of each PDF shows how the student did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the visible answer and say so.
- The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
- Auto-marked questions in the sim allowed one go each, so mark the answer exactly as printed.
- A blank question scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
- Some questions ask "How sure are you?" (Guessing, Fairly sure or Certain). Confidence is not marked. If a student was Certain but wrong, mention it kindly in the feedback: it shows an idea worth talking about.
- Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
- Mark each worksheet separately. An optional worksheet that wasn't submitted is not counted.

=== Force HQ Ch6 Part 4 (Compound machines) worksheet (17 marks) · MYP: A i, A ii ===
Q1 Sort it, 8 marks, 1 per item [A i]: Simple machine: A crowbar, A ramp, An axe, A single flagpole pulley. Compound machine: A pair of scissors, A building-site crane, A bicycle, A car jack with a handle.
Q2 Calculate, 2 marks [A ii]: 150 N (accept 149.5 to 150.5). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (N), 1 for correct working: Total mechanical advantage = 6 × 3 = 18, so the effort is 2,700 ÷ 18 = 150 N..
Q3 Answer and reason, 2 marks [A ii]: 1 mark for the answer: 25. 1 mark for the reason: The second machine multiplies a force the first machine has already made bigger. Confidence is not marked.
Q4 Put in order, 2 marks [A i]: The correct order is 1st D (The handle turns the winch's axle), 2nd A (The axle winds in the cable with a bigger force), 3rd B (The cable pulls on the pulley block), 4th C (The supporting ropes share the load and lift it). 2 marks if every letter is right; 1 mark if only two neighbouring letters are swapped; otherwise 0.
Q5 Explain it, 3 marks, 1 per point [A ii]: The question gives the words to use (compound machine, mechanical advantage, multiply, distance); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says the crane is a compound machine (two simple machines working together) • Says the total mechanical advantage is the two multiplied, so the effort is much smaller than the load • Says the hand must move much further than the beam rises (mechanical advantage × the load's distance) Model answer: The winch and the pulley system are two simple machines working together, so the crane is a compound machine. The winch makes the effort bigger, then the pulleys make it bigger again, so the total mechanical advantage is the two mechanical advantages multiplied together. That makes the effort much smaller than the beam's weight. The catch is distance: the operator's hand moves the total mechanical advantage times the height the beam rises, so the handle has to turn many times.
