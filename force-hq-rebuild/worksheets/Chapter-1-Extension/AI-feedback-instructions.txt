HOW TO MARK
- Student answers are printed in blue. Mark only what the student added. The worksheet's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
- Students don't need to match the answer key word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
- The feedback page at the end of each PDF shows how the student did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the visible answer and say so.
- The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
- Auto-marked questions in the sim allowed one go each, so mark the answer exactly as printed.
- A blank question scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
- Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
- Mark each worksheet separately. An optional worksheet that wasn't submitted is not counted.

=== Force HQ Ch1 Extension (What is a force?) worksheet (15 marks) · MYP: A i, A ii ===
Q1 Calculate, 2 marks [A ii]: 110 kg (accept 109.85 to 110.15). 1 mark for the answer, 1 for correct working: Mass = weight ÷ 9.8 = 1078 ÷ 9.8 = 110 kg.
Q2 Tick all that apply, 2 marks [A i]: Weight; Normal force; Tension (the pull of the rope); Friction. 2 marks for exactly these; 1 mark if one is missing or one extra is ticked; otherwise 0.
Q3 Draw it: free-body diagram, 6 marks [A ii]: 1 mark for each correct arrow (right force, right direction, right size): Weight 490 N down; Normal force 490 N up; Pull (tension) 130 N to the left; Pull (tension) 260 N to the right; Friction 49 N to the left. 1 more mark if bigger forces have longer arrows and equal forces have equal arrows. Arrows should start at the centre of the crate.
Q4 Choose one, 1 mark [A ii]: 1 cm.
Q5 Explain it, 4 marks, 1 per point [A ii]: The question gives the words to use (friction, against, balanced, unbalanced, resultant force); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says that on concrete, friction acts against the kid's pull • Says the pull and friction are balanced (the same size), so the resultant force is 0 N and the crate stays still • Says the air floor has no friction to act against the pull • Says that on the air floor the forces are unbalanced, so there is a resultant force and the crate starts to move Model answer: On rough concrete, friction acts against the kid's pull. Friction matches the 60 N pull, so the forces are balanced and the resultant force is 0 N. The crate stays still. The air floor has no friction, so nothing acts against the pull. The forces are unbalanced, so there is a resultant force and the crate starts to move.
