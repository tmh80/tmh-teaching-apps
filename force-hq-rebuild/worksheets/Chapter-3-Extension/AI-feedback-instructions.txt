=== Force HQ Ch3 Extension (F = ma) worksheet (13 marks) · MYP: A ii, A iii ===
Q1 Calculate, 2 marks [A ii]: 0.75 m/s² (accept 0.72 to 0.78). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (m/s²), 1 for correct working: Between 2 s and 6 s the velocity goes from 1.5 to 4.5 m/s: a change of 3 m/s in 4 s. a = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 m/s².
Q2 Choose one, 1 mark [A iii]: Line 2, because its velocity goes up by more every second.
Q3 Calculate, 2 marks [A ii]: 1234.8 N (accept 1228.63 to 1240.97). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (N), 1 for correct working: Mass = F ÷ a = 302.4 ÷ 2.4 = 126 kg. Weight = mass × 9.8 = 126 × 9.8 = 1234.8 N.
Q4 Draw it: free-body diagram, 5 marks [A ii]: 1 mark for each correct arrow (right force, right direction, right size): Weight 833 N down; Normal force 833 N up; Pull (tension) 270 N to the right; Friction 41.7 N to the left. 1 more mark if bigger forces have longer arrows and equal forces have equal arrows. Arrows should start at the centre of the crate.
Q5 Explain it, 3 marks, 1 per point [A ii]: The question gives the words to use (friction, resultant force, acceleration); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says there is more friction on the fresh powder • Says the resultant force (pull − friction) is smaller on the powder, even though the huskies pull just as hard • Uses F = ma to say that a smaller resultant force on the same mass gives a smaller acceleration Model answer: Fresh powder has more friction than packed snow. The huskies pull with the same force, but more of it is used up against the bigger friction, so the resultant force (pull − friction) is smaller. A smaller resultant force on the same mass gives a smaller acceleration, because a = F ÷ m.
