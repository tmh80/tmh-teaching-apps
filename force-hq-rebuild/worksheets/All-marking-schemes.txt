
Marking instructions for Toddle AI
This marking scheme is for the Force HQ worksheet "Chapter 1 Foundation: What is a force?". The blank template and the answer key for this worksheet are attached too.
• Compare the student's upload with the blank template and mark only what the student added. Student work is printed in blue. The template's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
• The answer key is the standard. Students don't need to match it word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
• The student's feedback page shows how they did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the worksheet and say so.
• The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". Students are expected to complete these steps before answering the questions. A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done; an empty box means it wasn't done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
• A blank question, or an empty template, scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
• Feedback for Grade 7–8: the score with a breakdown per question, one or two specific strengths quoting the student, and one next step with a hint rather than the answer. Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
Total: 28 marks.

Mark scheme
Q1 (5 marks, 1 per row): Weight → The Earth's gravity pulling on an object; Normal force → A surface pushing back on an object resting on it; Friction → Two surfaces rubbing or trying to slide past each other; Tension → A stretched rope or string pulling; Air resistance → Air pushing against something moving through it.
Q2 (7 marks, 1 per item): Contact force: Friction on a sliding sledge, The push of a foot on a football, The upward push of a chair on you, Water resistance on a swimmer. Non-contact force: The pull of a magnet on a paper clip, The Moon's gravity pulling on the oceans, The weight of a parachutist.
Q3 (2 marks): 230.3 N (accept 230.15 to 230.45). 1 mark for the answer, 1 for correct working: Weight = mass × 9.8 = 23.5 × 9.8 = 230.3 N.
Q4 (2 marks): 75 N (accept 74.5 to 75.5). 1 mark for the answer, 1 for correct working: Opposite directions, so subtract: 265 − 190 = 75 N (to the right).
Q5 (1 mark): Two forces act on it: its weight down and the normal force up, and they are the same size.
Q6 (3 marks, 1 per gap): gap 1: newtons (also accept newton, n); gap 2: 9.8 (also accept g, 9.8 n kg); gap 3: against (also accept opposite, opposite to, opposite way to, opposing, in the opposite direction to, opposite direction to).
Q7 (8 marks, 1 per cell): Unit: Mass = kilograms (kg), Weight = newtons (N); Is it a force?: Mass = No, it is not a force, Weight = Yes, it is a force; Measured with: Mass = a balance (scales), Weight = a newtonmeter; Changes on the Moon?: Mass = No, it stays the same, Weight = Yes, it gets smaller.
Total: 28 marks.

Mark scheme

Q3 (1 mark): Moving at a steady speed of 1.8 m/s.

Q6 (3 marks, 1 per point): The question gives the words to use (inertia, keeps moving, unbalanced force); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says the passenger was moving forwards at the same speed as the bus • Uses inertia to explain that the passenger keeps moving forwards when the bus slows down • States that an unbalanced force (from a pole, a seat or their feet) has to act on the passenger to bring them to a stop Model answer: The passenger was moving forwards at the same speed as the bus. When the bus brakes, the bus slows down, but nothing slows the passenger straight away. Because of their inertia, the passenger keeps moving forwards. To stop them, an unbalanced force has to act on them, such as the pull of a pole they grab or friction on their feet.

Mark scheme
Q1 (2 marks): 112.8 N (accept 112.24 to 113.36). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (N), 1 for correct working: F = m × a = 47 × 2.4 = 112.8 N.
Q2 (2 marks): 3.35 m/s² (accept 3.33 to 3.37). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (m/s²), 1 for correct working: a = F ÷ m = 134 ÷ 40 = 3.35 m/s².
Q3 (2 marks): 101 kg (accept 100.7 to 101.3). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (kg), 1 for correct working: m = F ÷ a = 121.2 ÷ 1.2 = 101 kg.
Q4 (1 mark): 3.2 m/s².
Q5 (3 marks, 1 per gap): gap 1: force (also accept any correct scientific word); gap 2: mass (also accept any correct scientific word); gap 3: acceleration (also accept any correct scientific word).
Q6 (2 marks): With the same pull, a heavier sled accelerates more slowly; A bigger resultant force on the same mass gives a bigger acceleration; With friction acting, the resultant force is the pull minus the friction. 2 marks for exactly these; 1 mark if one is missing or one extra is ticked; otherwise 0.
Q7 (2 marks): 2.64 m/s² (accept 2.62 to 2.66). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (m/s²), 1 for correct working: Resultant = 360 − 32.2 = 327.8 N. Then a = F ÷ m = 327.8 ÷ 124 = 2.64 m/s².
Q8 (3 marks, 1 per point): The question gives the words to use (resultant force, mass, acceleration); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says the resultant force is the same both times, because the huskies pull just as hard • Says the loaded sled has more mass • Uses F = ma (or the formula triangle) to explain that the same resultant force on a bigger mass gives a smaller acceleration Model answer: The two huskies pull with the same force both times, so the resultant force is the same. The loaded sled has more mass. Because F = m × a, the same resultant force on a bigger mass gives a smaller acceleration, so the loaded sled speeds up more slowly.

Mark scheme
Q1 (2 marks): 0.75 m/s² (accept 0.72 to 0.78). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (m/s²), 1 for correct working: Between 2 s and 6 s the speed goes from 1.5 to 4.5 m/s: a change of 3 m/s in 4 s. a = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 m/s².
Q2 (1 mark): Line 2, because its speed goes up by more every second.
Q3 (2 marks): 3.21 m/s² (accept 3.19 to 3.23). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (m/s²), 1 for correct working: Resultant = 450 − 49 = 401 N. Then a = F ÷ m = 401 ÷ 125 = 3.21 m/s².
Q4 (2 marks): 1234.8 N (accept 1228.63 to 1240.97). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (N), 1 for correct working: Mass = F ÷ a = 302.4 ÷ 2.4 = 126 kg. Weight = mass × 9.8 = 126 × 9.8 = 1234.8 N.
Q5 (5 marks): 1 mark for each correct arrow (right force, right direction, right size): Weight 833 N down; Normal force 833 N up; Pull (tension) 270 N to the right; Friction 41.7 N to the left. 1 more mark if bigger forces have longer arrows and equal forces have equal arrows. Arrows should start at the centre of the crate.
Q6 (3 marks, 1 per point): The question gives the words to use (friction, resultant force, acceleration); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says there is more friction on the fresh powder • Says the resultant force (pull − friction) is smaller on the powder, even though the huskies pull just as hard • Uses F = ma to say that a smaller resultant force on the same mass gives a smaller acceleration Model answer: Fresh powder has more friction than packed snow. The huskies pull with the same force, but more of it is used up against the bigger friction, so the resultant force (pull − friction) is smaller. A smaller resultant force on the same mass gives a smaller acceleration, because a = F ÷ m.
