HOW TO MARK
- Student answers are printed in blue. Mark only what the student added. The worksheet's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
- Students don't need to match the answer key word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
- The feedback page at the end of each PDF shows how the student did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the visible answer and say so.
- The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
- Auto-marked questions in the sim allowed one go each, so mark the answer exactly as printed.
- A blank question scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
- Some questions ask "How sure are you?" (Guessing, Fairly sure or Certain). Confidence is not marked. If a student was Certain but wrong, mention it kindly in the feedback: it shows an idea worth talking about.
- The top of the worksheet shows "My self-check before the Check". It is the student's own self-review: for each I can statement they placed an X on a line from Beginning to Developing, Proficient and Mastering. It is not marked and earns no marks, so do not grade or correct it. In the feedback you may add at most one short sentence comparing how sure they felt with how they did.
- Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
- Mark each worksheet separately. An optional worksheet that wasn't submitted is not counted.

Feedback for Grade 7–8: the score with a breakdown per question, one or two specific strengths quoting the student, and one next step with a hint rather than the answer.

For each criterion, add the marks earned and the marks available across all the worksheets, work out the percentage, then give the level: 1–12% = level 1, 13–25% = level 2, 26–37% = level 3, 38–50% = level 4, 51–62% = level 5, 63–75% = level 6, 76–87% = level 7, 88–100% = level 8 (0% = level 0).
Check the level against the MYP band (1–2 limited, 3–4 adequate, 5–6 substantial, 7–8 excellent). You may move it by one level if the student's work clearly fits a different band; say why if you do. Q1 Match it up, 6 marks, 1 per row [A i]: Flagpole → Pulley; Screwdriver → Wheel and axle; Wheelchair ramp → Ramp (inclined plane); Doorstop → Wedge; Jar lid → Screw; Seesaw → Lever.
Q2 Choose the words, 2 marks, 1 per choice [A ii]: A crane lifts a load with 4 supporting ropes. Compared with lifting the load straight up, the effort is [a quarter as big] and the rope pulled is [four times longer].. 1 mark for each right choice.
Q3 Spot the mistake, 2 marks [A iii]: 1 mark for the line with the mistake: line 4 (A steeper ramp would need a smaller pull, because it is shorter.). 1 mark for the correction: A steeper ramp would need a bigger pull, but over a shorter distance. A shorter ramp to the same height needs a bigger pull, but the box travels a shorter distance.
Q4 Answer and reason, 2 marks [A ii]: 1 mark for the answer: It gets smaller. 1 mark for the reason: Her hand moves round a bigger circle, so a smaller force does the same job. Confidence is not marked.
Q5 Explain it, 3 marks, 1 per point [A ii]: The question gives the words to use (wedge, force, thin, distance); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Says the sloping sides push the ice apart with a bigger force than the push • Says a long, thin wedge needs a smaller push than a short, fat one • Says the smaller push has to move further (the catch) Model answer: A wedge is two ramps back to back. When Smooshy pushes the wedge into the ice, its sloping sides push the two halves apart with a much bigger force than his push. He should choose a long, thin wedge, because it needs the smallest push. The catch is that he has to push it further into the ice.
