HOW TO MARK
- Student answers are printed in blue. Mark only what the student added. The worksheet's own text, choices and word lists are not student work.
- Students don't need to match the answer key word for word: accept any correct scientific wording.
- The feedback page at the end of each PDF shows how the student did in the sim. If it disagrees with what you can see on the worksheet, trust the visible answer and say so.
- The worksheet starts with "Steps completed in the sim". A blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done. These ticks are not marked, but mention any skipped steps in the feedback.
- Auto-marked questions in the sim allowed one go each, so mark the answer exactly as printed.
- A blank question scores 0. Add a kind note saying what is missing.
- Some questions ask "How sure are you?" (Guessing, Fairly sure or Certain). Confidence is not marked. If a student was Certain but wrong, mention it kindly in the feedback: it shows an idea worth talking about.
- The top of the worksheet shows "My self-check before the Check". It is the student's own self-review: for each I can statement they placed an X on a line from Beginning to Developing, Proficient and Mastering. It is not marked and earns no marks, so do not grade or correct it. In the feedback you may add at most one short sentence comparing how sure they felt with how they did.
- Don't take marks off for spelling unless the meaning is unclear.
- Mark each worksheet separately. An optional worksheet that wasn't submitted is not counted.

=== Force HQ Ch6 Part 5 (Rescue Captain Smooshy!) worksheet (17 marks) · MYP: A ii, A iii, D i, D ii, D iii ===
Q1 Explain it, 3 marks, 1 per point [A ii, D i, D iii]: The question gives the words to use (winch, pulley, mechanical advantage, effort); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Names the winch (wheel and axle) and the pulley block as the two simple machines • Says the mechanical advantages multiply, so a small effort moves the heavy car • Says the cable moves much further than the car (the trade-off) Model answer: The winch is a wheel and axle that winds in the cable, and the pulley block adds supporting ropes. Together they make a compound machine, so their mechanical advantages multiply. That means a small effort from the winch's motor or handle can pull the heavy car out. The cable has to be wound in much further than the car moves.
Q2 Calculate, 2 marks [A ii]: 94,500 J (accept 94,490 to 94,510). 1 mark for the answer with its unit (J), 1 for correct working: Useful work = 6,300 × 15 = 94,500 J, for every route that worked. Smooshy does some extra work on top of this whenever the Gumdrop drags or rolls over the ground..
Q3 Answer and reason, 2 marks [A iii]: 1 mark for the answer: No, the work stays the same, but the effort gets smaller. 1 mark for the reason: He pulls with less force over a longer distance, so the work needed to lift the Gumdrop stays at 94,500 J. Confidence is not marked.
Q4 Explain it, 5 marks, 1 per point [D ii, D iii]: The question gives the words to use (mechanical advantage, distance, safety, cost); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Gives an advantage of Plan A (the ramps' mechanical advantage means a smaller or cheaper crane, or a smaller effort) • Gives a disadvantage of Plan A (more cable or distance, or more time) • Gives an advantage of Plan B (shorter distance, quicker, or less damage to the ground) • Gives a disadvantage of Plan B (needs a bigger mechanical advantage or a more expensive crane, or more dangerous if it fails) • Makes a choice and gives a reason for it Model answer: Plan A uses the ramps, which give mechanical advantage, so a smaller, cheaper crane and a smaller effort are enough. But it needs much more cable and a longer haul, and building the ramps takes time. Plan B moves the ship a much shorter distance, so it is quicker and hardly disturbs the ground. But the crane needs a much bigger mechanical advantage of its own, so it costs more to build and fly there, and a heavy ship hanging high on one cable is more dangerous if something breaks. I would choose Plan A, because it is safer and cheaper, even though it takes longer.
Q5 Explain it, 5 marks, 1 per point [A ii, D i, D iii]: The question gives the words to use (load, effort, mechanical advantage, distance, work); each point is about using one of them correctly. • Names the machines in the route, in order • Gives the total mechanical advantage of the hardest leg and says the parts multiply • Compares the pull with Smooshy's 60 N • Explains that a smaller effort means a longer hand distance • Gives one improvement with a reason Model answer: My route used a ramp, a pulley block and a winch on each leg. On my hardest leg the 30 m ramp rose 6 m, so the ramp's mechanical advantage was 30 ÷ 6 = 5. With 4 ropes and a ×7 winch the parts multiply, so the total mechanical advantage was 5 × 4 × 7 = 140. Smooshy's effort was the load ÷ the mechanical advantage = 6,300 ÷ 140 = 45 N, which is under his 60 N limit, so the thrusters stayed off. The price is distance: his hand moved 140 × 6 = 840 m on that leg. The work was still 6,300 × 6 = 37,800 J, because machines change the effort and the distance but not the work. Next time I would blast the crest lower, because then the ship needs to be lifted less far, which means less work and less hand travel. I also noticed that lifting with ramps and machines kept the hull safe, but dragging the Gumdrop over rough ground scraped the hull, could tip it over, and needed far more hand travel. Lifting is much kinder to the ship than dragging. Accept any route and mark the numbers against the student's My Rescue table (page 1 of the worksheet). Credit a student who compares lifting with dragging: dragging scrapes the hull and can tip the Gumdrop over, while ramps and lifts do not.
My Rescue page (not marked): Page 1 of the worksheet has a My Rescue page: a picture of the student's best rescue route, the result and score out of 11, a row for each leg (machines and move, force needed, total MA, Smooshy's pull, hand travel) and totals for pieces, crystals, hand travel, hull damage and hints. The rows are the student's own working from the route strip: Smooshy's pull = force needed ÷ total MA, and hand travel = total MA × the distance the Gumdrop moves. GASA approves the maths before the rescue launches, and the hints used are shown. The score is 4 for reaching the line, 2 for a spotless hull (1 under 20% damage), 2 for 475 crystals or fewer, 1 for 2,500 m of hand travel or less, 1 for 8 pieces or fewer and 1 for maths approved by GASA. Before the rescue, a training round (a 252 N rock cleared with a lever and a crane) is not scored and is not shown on the page. It is not marked, because Challenges are not marked, but you may mention it in a strength or the next step. The last question is a reflection on their own route: check the numbers they quote against the My Rescue table.
