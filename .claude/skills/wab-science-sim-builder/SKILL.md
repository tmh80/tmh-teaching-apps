# WAB Science Sim Builder

For open-ended science simulations in the family of Force HQ (G7/8 forces), Sparks Through Time (G6 electricity) and Adapt Lab (G7/8 adaptations/evolution). Formative virtual labs where students display knowledge in a lab report (like Paper Plane Lab) use `wab-virtual-lab-builder` instead. Simulations are for free play, discovery, foundation building and extension; virtual labs are clear steps in order so students get feedback.

Tom is Head of MS Sciences at WAB (Beijing). Grade 6 is one class; Grade 7/8 is a mixed class on Cycle A / Cycle B.

## 1. The core design rule: students use it alone

Students open the sim at home or in class with no teacher beside them. Every design decision follows from that.

- **Always obvious what to do next.** Each screen has one clear next action. No dead ends, no hidden controls, no step that needs a teacher to explain.
- **Hint ladder, never a bare answer.** Wrong or stuck → gentle nudge → stronger hint pointing at the sim → worked example. The student still does the final step.
- **Feedback explains why.** Every check says what was right or wrong and links back to what the sim showed.
- **Predict before running.** Students lock in a prediction (choice, slider or sketch), run the sim, then compare and explain. Never show the result first.
- **Safe to experiment.** Reset buttons everywhere, undo where possible, no way to break or lose progress. Silly inputs give a fun, informative response rather than an error.
- **Explore mode prompts real experiments.** Offer "try this" investigations (change one variable, keep the others the same, record what happens) so free play turns into fair testing.
- **Catch misconceptions.** Where a common wrong idea exists (heavier falls faster, current gets used up), design a prediction that reveals it and a run that challenges it.
- **Evidence of work for the teacher.** Because Tom isn't watching, the student's answers, sketches, builds and progress export to a worksheet or build sheet (sections 4b and 4e).
- **Sections of 20–30 minutes that can be combined.** Split every chapter into self-contained sections (e.g. Foundation, Extension) of about 20–30 minutes. Each section has its own Learn steps, optional Challenge, Check questions, stamp and worksheet PDF, so a teacher can set one on its own or several together for a longer session. Every time shown to students or teachers says "approx." (e.g. "approx. 28 min"), estimated from the steps and question types. A finished section offers the next one in a "keep going" box. Progress saves automatically.
- **Something to do in every step.** No step is just a run of info cards. Every Learn step has at least one "Do this" job in the sim and at least one question, even if it is a quick comprehension check on the cards just read, so the flow never stalls.
- **Vary the situations.** Don't let a section's questions all use the same set-up (e.g. every crate sitting still). Mix still and moving, balanced and unbalanced, small and large values.
- **Clues for multi-step reasoning.** Where a question or challenge needs a step students haven't been walked through (e.g. rearranging weight = mass × 9.8 to find the mass), add a "Need a clue?" fold-out that asks a guiding question rather than giving the method ("If you multiply mass by 9.8 to get the weight, what do you need to do to the weight and 9.8 to get the mass?").
- **Do it before you're told it.** Let students try the thing first (rub the balloon on hair, then on a jumper, then hold it near paper), ask what they observed (an observation question for each test), and only then explain the science. Introduce every idea and word before any question or instruction relies on it (e.g. teach static vs current before a mission that uses them; don't ask for a "complete path" before it has been taught).
- **Key ideas are impossible to miss, and students have to use them.** Any text that carries a learning outcome (the main idea of a step, a definition, a rule) is the most eye-catching text on the screen, never the smallest or palest. Learn-step instructions go in a large speech bubble (about 18px, slightly smaller on phones), with a tinted background, a strong coloured border and the key words in bold colour. Then make students act on the key idea so it can't be skimmed, choosing what fits the step:
  - a **pop-out "Key idea" box** that stays open until the student answers a quick multiple-choice question about it;
  - **"Tap the sentence that…"**: show the key text as 3–5 tappable sentences and ask students to tap the one that matches a paraphrase of the concept (e.g. "Tap the sentence that explains why the bulb went out"), with a hint after a wrong tap;
  - a **key-word gap** the student types into.
  Every key idea behind the unit's learning outcomes and standards gets one of these (the standards themselves stay off student screens; map them during planning). Answers are saved with the rest of the Learn answers.
- **Answers are remembered.** Learn/guided answers are saved and shown again when a student returns, with a "Try this step again" button. A "📄 Save my work" PDF button sits in the tab bar so students can save their progress at any time.
- **Never soft-lock.** A student must always be able to recover from a bad choice: undo, remove for a refund (things built this round), sell older items for part of their value, redo a round, go back a round, and start again from the beginning (two-tap confirm). Never leave them repeating a round they can't win.

## 2. Standing preferences

- **Guided mode first, then explore mode.** Guided teaches the basics step by step, introducing each panel slowly; explore shows everything. Explore opens once the first chapter's Foundation section is finished: until then its home card says when it opens, with a "Start Chapter 1" button and an "Open it anyway" button (never a hard block). The stamp pop-up for that section announces "Explore is now open!" with an "Open Explore" button. Teacher mode always gets in.
- **Active, never passive.** Students predict, reason, build and test. Avoid "read this, click next" sequences anywhere in the sim.
- **Challenges use the sim itself.** Build, draw or design something and compare it to an exemplar or a target. Avoid plain quizzes as the main challenge.
- **Open builders in every sim** (section 4e): free, drag-and-drop building of the unit's key things (a circuit, an atom, a power grid on a map, a food web, a machine), each with template build tasks that can be set as classwork or homework.
- **Questions are varied, plentiful and not obvious** (sections 4a-2 and 4a-3): every section ends with a Check (Review) of about 5–10 questions in mixed formats, sized to fit the section's 20–30 minutes, never a run of easy multiple choice.
- **Free-form builders.** Where students build something, make it free-form and drag-and-drop like PhET (parts bin, stretchy wires, snapping ends, drag onto a map) rather than fixed grid slots.
- **Teacher "set classwork or homework" page in every sim** (section 4c), reached from a button at the top of the home screen, with Toddle AI marking support (section 4d).
- **Voice.** Enthusiastic, warm and encouraging, in natural sentences. Avoid clipped, punchy, AI-sounding lines. Read every block of student text aloud in your head before shipping.
- **Short and simple, because reading stamina is low (especially Grade 6).** Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence, explanations of two or three sentences at most. Use numbered steps for instructions. Put detail behind a "How do I read this?" or "Your mission" fold-out instead of on screen. Key terms that remain get glossary hover/tap definitions (section 4).
- **Words that mean the same thing everywhere.** WAB students come from many countries, so avoid words that mean something different in UK and US English or that suggest something else. A student read "torch" as a burning stick, so Sparks says "flashlight". When a regional word is still the clearest choice, add a glossary entry that names the other version ("In the UK it is often called a torch"). Tom is happy with "jumper".
- **Science conventions.** g = 9.8 N/kg (never 10); give students a pop-out calculator for it. Free-body diagrams show the actual object, with weight and normal force drawn from the centre. Use non-round, varied numbers in scenarios so answers aren't guessable.
- **Purpose.** Sims are review, extension and support for a unit, not lesson-by-lesson first teaching. Summative tasks are handled separately.
- **Standards** (ACARA, MYP criteria) are for Tom's planning only. Keep them off every student-facing screen, worksheet and export. Worksheets don't need "I can" statements.
- **Originality.** Each sim gets its own concept and metaphor (Force HQ = map of lab buildings; Sparks = time travel with a time machine). Don't reuse another sim's structure. Offer Tom 3–4 concept options and let him choose before building.
- **Realism.** Physics and animation should look right: smoothed force ramps, sensible graph axes, scenes that stay in one believable setting.

## 3. Workflow

1. **Ground it.** Ask for (or read from the project) the unit overview and lesson sequence. Pull out the key concepts, vocabulary and likely misconceptions.
2. **Concept options.** Propose 3–4 distinct concepts in a few lines each. Wait for Tom's choice.
3. **Science first for big features.** For any new model or engine, explain the science and how it will be modelled before writing code. Wait for a go-ahead.
4. **Plan the stops/labs.** For each: the key ideas tied to the learning outcomes and how each one is made prominent and checked (section 1), what students predict, what they explore, the challenge, the misconceptions targeted, the question plan (section 4a-3: purpose → type → Learn or Check), the worksheet, and the key vocabulary and goal used on the teacher page. Plan the open builders and their build tasks too.
5. **Build in stages** using the shared shell (section 4) and the file layout (section 5).
6. **Test** (section 6) before every delivery, including feasibility checks for every build task.
7. **Deliver** (section 7), including the AI feedback text and, for records, the printable files (blank template, answer key and marking scheme for every sheet).
8. **Hand off.** At the end of a session, update the sim's brief and backlog (section 8) so the next chat starts cold without a catch-up.

When Tom batches feedback (a list, or artifact comments), make a task list from it, address every item, and report briefly on each.

## 4. The shared lab shell

Reuse these components across sims instead of rebuilding them. If a `shell/` folder already exists, copy it in and adapt the theme; if not, build them as reusable modules the first time.

- **Info cards:** small dismissible cards over the scene that deliver teaching content.
- **Journal:** every info card and key result is saved to a persistent journal the student can reopen from anywhere.
- **Question box ("Your turn"):** Learn questions stay hidden while the student does the step. The Next button reads "Next: questions →"; pressing it with the Do this list unfinished shows a warning with "Continue anyway", then the questions pop up in a box. The box has "▾ Minimise" (Escape does the same), which docks it as a small "Your turn ⤢ Open" tab in the side column, and its footer has "← Back to the arena" and the next-step button. Diagram questions minimise it automatically. Answers can be typed or drawn.
- **Every pop-up is draggable and never blocks the sim.** Any pop-up that can cover the simulation (the question box, info cards, how-to-read cards) is dragged by its header bar (a ⠿ grip, `cursor: grab`, `touch-action: none`) using pointer events, so mouse, pen and touch all work. Use one shared helper (`makeDraggable(box, handleSelector, key)`) for all of them. The layer behind a pop-up is transparent and click-through (`pointer-events: none` on the layer, `auto` on the box), so students can read meters and use controls with the questions open. The header always stays on screen (clamp the box so its header can't leave the viewport, measuring from the box top to the header bottom), the box keeps its spot while the page is open (also for the next card), and it re-clamps on resize. Buttons inside the header still work. Modals that genuinely need an answer (the name box, warnings) stay as normal modals. Test at phone width, where a pop-up is nearly full screen and dragging it down is how students uncover the sim.
- **Every slider has a typed number box.** Beside each slider, a small number input takes any value the slider allows (applied on Enter or when the box loses focus, clamped to the slider's range), so students never fight a slider to hit an exact value.
- **Target-finding gets too-high / too-low markers.** When a task asks students to find a value by trial and error (a brake force that stops a crate on a mark), each try leaves a marker on the slider: red for a value that was too high, yellow for one that was too low, keeping only the closest of each, with a note under the slider ("too low: 48 N · too high: 150 N. The right value is between them.").
- **Labels stay on screen.** Readings drawn on a canvas (meters, labels, pills) are clamped inside the canvas in every state (e.g. when an object reaches the end of its track), so a question that reads them can always be answered.
- **Journal = a side panel with three tabs: Runs, Notes, Vocab.** The Journal opens beside the sim (on screens 1180 px and wider it pushes the page aside instead of covering it), so students can read it while they use the sim and answer questions. The header Journal button toggles it.
  - **📋 Runs (run history):** every action that runs the sim (pull, push, launch, drop, build test) starts a run that updates live while it plays and is then saved, with one plain sentence ("You pushed the 24 kg crate on the air floor. It glided at a steady 3.13 m/s all the way to the end of the arena.") and chips for the key numbers (mass, surface, forces, top speed, distance, stopping distance, time). Runs from the current step are open; earlier runs fold to a one-line summary the student can open. A question that asks for a value from an earlier run must never force the student to run the experiment again; questions still check against the student's own recorded runs.
  - **📝 Notes:** the saved info cards with the student's own notes and questions, plus Evidence of my learning.
  - **🔤 Vocab:** every glossary word the student has met (counted once it has actually been on screen, or its definition opened), A to Z, with its meaning, where it was first met and a search box. This feeds the flashcards planned for every sim and lab.
  - The Your turn box has one big **"📋 See my runs"** button (with a count) that opens the Journal on the Runs tab; don't list runs inside the question box. Introduce the Journal and its tabs in the first chapter's first step, with a Do this job to open the Runs tab.
- **Table maker and graph maker:** for investigations where students collect their own numbers, a journal table (typed beside the sim) and a graph window where they drag each point or bar into place, like the Paper Plane Lab graph tool. Both can be chosen as evidence and print on the worksheet. See section 4f.
- **Drawing pad:** pressure-sensitive sketch pad for diagrams (free-body diagrams, circuit sketches, models), with pens, eraser, undo and clear. Saved as strokes plus a PNG for worksheets.
- **Calculator:** a floating "🧮 Calculator" button on **every screen** (bottom-right, icon-only on phones, hidden in print; always visible above the Journal and other side panels, moved clear of an open panel) that pops out a draggable calculator: big display with the last sum shown above, keys C ( ) ⌫ 0–9 . ÷ × − + =, typing and Enter supported (typing x or * shows ×, / shows ÷), friendly errors ("You can't divide by zero."). Evaluate with a small safe parser, never `eval`. Add a formula-triangle helper where relevant (F = m × a, V = I × R, W = F × d).
- **Glossary:** key terms (about 50–80 per sim, including terms used in questions such as "whole-life CO₂" or "kWh") are automatically underlined with a dotted line wherever they appear (first use per card/question, via a MutationObserver over the app) and show a one- or two-sentence definition on hover (mouse), focus, or tap (touch, with a short toggle guard so the tap doesn't immediately close it). Hidden in print.
- **Fun facts:** clickable facts tied to concepts already met.
- **Onboarding tour:** first-run tour highlighting journal, calculator, glossary, question box and modes. Replayable.
- **Progress and rewards:** badges/stamps/XP, with a visible map of what's done and what's next. Show the stamp images themselves (on the home screen and when earned, with a stamp-down animation). Follow section 4a.
- **Review engine, worksheets, teacher page, AI marking and open builders:** sections 4a-2 to 4e.
- **Who's using it:** ask for the student's **name and class at the very start** (a simple modal before the home screen). **Nobody gets in without a name or the teacher code:** closing the box (Cancel, Escape or clicking outside) sends them back to it. Build the box as plain divs with click and Enter handlers rather than a `<form>`, because sandboxed previews block form submission. Show the name at the top left of every screen as a chip with a "Change" button. Worksheets and build sheets **can't be saved as PDF without a name and class** (the print view shows "No name, no worksheet!"): this discourages copying. An "I'm a teacher" option asks for the teacher code (section 4d); teacher mode unlocks every stop, step and chapter and lets PDFs save without a name, with a "Switch to student mode" button so Tom can play through as a student and switch back.
- **Save:** progress and answers stored in localStorage, every read/write wrapped in try/catch, and the sim must work fully if storage is unavailable.
- **Layout:** controls docked beside or under the simulation so students rarely scroll. Big game-like activities get a **wide layout** (up to ~1500 px, simulation left and controls right) and a **focus mode** that hides the surrounding page chrome; put the briefing in a collapsible "📋 Your mission" panel (open only before the first attempt) and results in **tabs** ("What happened", "Scorecard", "Money") in a fixed-height scroll box. Keep the main action button and every "what next" button (next round, redo, back, start again, undo) **together in one row**. Test desktop, iPad and phone widths. Support touch and mouse.
- **Actions appear where you tap.** In builders and boards, tapping a part, connection or junction opens a small pop-up menu right beside it (positioned at the tap point, kept inside the board) with only the actions for that thing, clear icons (✂ Cut / ✂ Disconnect here, ➕ Add a joining point, Turn it around, Remove) and a ✕ to close. No action bar tucked away below the board. Put one line under the board: "Tap a part, a wire or a dot to see what you can do with it."
- **Hidden elements:** add `[hidden]{display:none !important}` to the stylesheet so a component's own `display` rule never reveals something meant to stay hidden (for example a model answer before the student has tried).

## 4a. Progress, freedom and measuring (learned from classroom use)

**Make progress obvious**
- **Every task shows its state, loudly.** Done = green "✓ Done" pill and border; not done = amber "To do". The map/stops show done (green), part-done (amber) and not started.
- **"On this page" bar** at the top of each screen ("2 of 5 done") with chips that scroll to each task.
- **Done means complete, never correct.** A task turns green when every box has something in it or it reaches a sensible minimum; say under each task what makes it turn green.
- **Prerequisites stated exactly** ("You need to finish the Drop Lab first: 2 runs to go") with a "Go to …" button.

**Freedom**
- **Block only what the next activity can't run without.** Everything else warns with "Continue anyway". Never disable a Next button for unfinished but non-essential work.
- **Order of a section:** Learn → Challenge (optional) → Check. The Check (and its worksheet) opens once the Challenge is complete, or once Learn is complete if the section has no Challenge, with a "Continue anyway" option, so the worksheet shows understanding built in the sim.
- **Point students to the Check after Learn.** Explore and other free play can sit between Learn and the Check, and students who wander off never make their worksheet. When Learn finishes, the stamp pop-up says the Check comes next and makes the worksheet they hand in on Toddle, with "Start the review →" (primary) and "Explore first". Explore shows a "📄 Don't forget your worksheet!" banner with a "Go to the review" button until that Check is complete (not in teacher mode). Finishing the last Challenge mission does the same for its own review.
- **Make each finished step look finished:** a green tick, a "Step complete" banner and a clearly highlighted Next button.
- When something essential is missing, take the student straight to it, outline it in red and explain in a pop-up (shown after any intro pop-up).

**Measuring by hand**
- Let students measure for themselves where possible (read scales, time events, calculate), with simple aids: zoomed scales, countdowns, the needed control highlighted, the reading shown beside the entry box, a keep-or-redo choice, a calculator. Once the student has typed a value, the sim can check it and explain, because sims are for learning.
- **Students type every reading. Never auto-fill a results table.** When a meter or scale shows a value (ammeter, voltmeter, stopwatch, force meter), the step isn't complete until the student reads it and types it in. Use a reading table: each row unlocks once that set-up is on screen, the student types the value, and the sim checks it within a small tolerance ("Look again at the meter. Copy the number exactly.") before ticking the row. Saved like other Learn answers. Students should never be passengers.
- **Collect data where the sim is, never behind "Next".** Anything a student has to read, measure or record from the arena must be on screen next to the arena the moment the step opens, and must be typeable straight away. Three rules:
  - **A Do this task never points at a question that only opens later** (for example "Answer the key idea question in the Your turn box, which opens when you press Next: questions"). The unfinished-tasks warning counts it as undone and sends the student round in a circle. Questions that are not about the sim stay in the Your turn box and are not listed as tasks.
  - **Numbers read from the arena get an inline answer box in the Do this list** (task `[html, fn, {q:'q1'}]`) with its own Check button. What the student types copies into the matching Your turn question, so they type once. Use it for every value they read off the sim: settled velocity, fall time, a weight on a scale.
  - **Tables the student fills while the sim runs sit under the arena** (`early: true` on the question, shown in a "Collect your data here" card), not in the Your turn box. Each row says when to act ("Pause in the first second of the fall") so nobody has to ask which rows or where.
- **No "Use this moment" buttons.** Pausing at the moment a row describes fills that row in by itself (the first empty row whose "when" fits), with a toast, and a small "Pick another moment" button to redo a row. A button that can say "no moment attached" is one more thing to get wrong.
- **Never jump the page or steal keyboard focus while a student is using a control** (for example a running stopwatch). Use buttons to move between the experiment and the results ("📋 Write it in my table ↓", "⬆ Back to the experiment"). Show only the tools a measurement needs.

## 4a-2. Review questions (every section)

Each section/stop ends with a **Check** tab (the Review; unlocked after the Challenge, or after Learn if there is no Challenge) holding **about 5–10 questions**, sized to fit the section's 20–30 minutes, defined as data in one content module so the same items drive the sim, the final mixed review, the worksheets, the answer keys and the marking schemes.

- **Mix the formats in every section.** Pick types from the question bank and write the section's question plan first (section 4a-3): at least five different types per Check, chosen by what each question is for.
- **Every question has a model answer** (draw: an exemplar drawing; explain: a written model answer; calculate: a worked explanation) and every draw/explain item has a checklist of 2–4 points: these feed the answer key and become the marks in the marking scheme (section 4d). Check this with a script before shipping.
- **Make them think.** Prefer application, data interpretation, diagrams, prediction and misconception items over recall. Never make the first option always the right one (shuffle in the sim, seeded shuffle on paper).
- **No specificity pattern in choice questions.** Students must not be able to pick the answer because it is the longest or most specific option. Every option in a choose-one, tap-the-sentence or tick question is about as long and as detailed as the right one, and distractors reuse the same numbers and words (for a flat line at 1.8 m/s: "Moving at a steady 1.8 m/s", "Speeding up to 1.8 m/s", "Slowing down to 1.8 m/s", "Standing still, 1.8 m from the start"). Check every choice question before shipping.
- **Explain questions give the words to use.** Each explain item lists "Words to use in a good answer" (e.g. inertia, keeps moving, unbalanced force), shown in the sim and on the worksheet. Each checklist point says how one of those words should be used ("States that an unbalanced force has to act on the passenger to bring them to a stop"), and the marking scheme says the points are about using the given words correctly.
- **Scaffold every calculation.** Any maths a Check question needs (a ratio, a rearranged formula, "n times the mass gives 1/n of the speed") is first shown as a pattern in the sim, then as a worked example, then practised in Learn with a hint ladder, before the Check uses it. Break multi-step reasoning into steps in Learn (how many times heavier? how many times further? so what force?).
- **One go per question.** Every auto-marked question (in Learn, the Challenge and the Check) locks after its first check, right or wrong. A wrong answer shows the right one ("Whoops! The answer is …", followed by the reason): choose-one questions mark the right option with a dashed green outline; sort, match, table and gap questions put a green "→ correct" tag beside each wrong part. Say under each card: "You get one go, so check your answer carefully before you press Check." Diagram builders get one check, then the diagram locks and "Your diagram" is shown beside "The correct diagram" with ✓/✗ chips. "Try this step again" in Learn still resets a whole step.
- **Points per part.** Score multi-part questions per part so one slip doesn't lose everything: choose one, tap, calculate and reading 1; tick all 2 (minus one per mistake); gap, sort and match 1 per gap, item or row; table 1 per cell; diagrams 1 per builder check (e.g. 6); explain 1 per self-ticked checklist point; challenge missions per objective met. Show "Points so far: x of y" on the Check page, "n of m points" on each card, and points per question on the worksheet's feedback page.
- **Accept synonyms in typed answers.** Typed gaps and key words accept sensible synonyms through one shared list of word groups, so every word in a group counts when one of them is accepted (bigger / more / larger / greater / stronger; smaller / less / weaker; centre / center / middle; against / opposite / opposing; newtons / N). Ignore case, punctuation and a leading "a", "an" or "the". The marking scheme lists every accepted word for each gap.
- **Every Check question is taught first.** Before shipping, check each Check question against the Learn steps (and Challenge) of its own section. If a skill (e.g. drawing to scale) isn't taught, add a card and a practice question to Learn, or change the question.
- **Card states:** each question is a card with type label, To do/Done pill (showing its points once done), its own Check button and explained feedback. A sticky bar shows "x of N done", the points so far and chips that jump to each card.
- **Final mixed review** (e.g. Sparks' Time Test) draws auto-marked items from every section's Review rather than a separate easy MCQ bank.
- **MYP strands on every question (teacher-only).** Every Check item, build-task question and rubric row carries `obj:[...]`, the MYP Sciences strands it assesses. A sheet's strands are the union of its items' tags. Grade 6 is MYP Year 1:
  | Strand | Year 1 wording | Tag questions like this |
  |---|---|---|
  | A i | outline scientific knowledge | recall: name, match, sort into taught groups, fill gaps, label, order known events, explain a taught idea |
  | A ii | apply scientific knowledge and understanding to solve problems set in familiar situations | calculations, predicting a new set-up with a rule, diagnosing a fault, drawing to a spec |
  | A iii | apply information to make judgments | using given data, results or evidence to reach a conclusion; judging a claim; choosing the best option |
  | D i | summarize the ways in which science is applied and used to address a specific problem or issue | real uses: lightning rods, cranes, recycling, house wiring, the grid, power stations |
  | D ii | describe and summarize the various implications of using science and its application in solving a specific problem or issue | safety, cost, energy wasted, CO₂ and other environmental effects |
  | D iii | apply scientific language effectively | explain-it questions with "Words to use", only on sheets that also carry D i or D ii |

  Only some strands apply to each sheet; tag what the questions really assess rather than spreading every strand everywhere. For the mixed Grade 7/8 class, ask Tom which year's strand wording to use before tagging the first G7/8 sim. Strands appear only on the answer key, the marking scheme, the teacher page and the Toddle text; never on student screens, student worksheets or blank templates. A check script fails if any item has no `obj` and prints each sheet's strands.
- **Marks to MYP levels.** A question's marks count towards each criterion it is tagged with (once per criterion). Per criterion, earned ÷ available gives a level through one constant Tom can edit: `const LEVEL_CUTS = [1, 13, 26, 38, 51, 63, 76, 88];` (lowest percentage for levels 1 to 8; 0% is level 0). The AI feedback text tells Toddle to check the level against the MYP bands (1–2 limited, 3–4 adequate, 5–6 substantial, 7–8 excellent), allows a one-level adjustment with a reason, and to report "Criterion A: level x/8 (strands assessed: …)" with a sentence per strand.

## 4a-3. Question bank (pick types by what each question is for)

Plan questions from their purpose, then pick a type from this bank. The aim is that every section tests understanding from several angles (recall, organising ideas, misconceptions, observation, maths, producing), so students can't get through on recognition alone.

**Design rules from the research (apply to every type)**
- **Every wrong option means something.** Each distractor matches one named misconception, a question tests one idea, and a student who holds the misconception can't get it right by luck (Craig Barton's diagnostic-question rules; hinge questions).
- **Ask for the reason too.** Two-tier questions (answer, then reason) separate understanding from a lucky guess; students often pick the right answer for the wrong reason.
- **Predict, observe, explain.** Simulation-supported POE beats plain POE, so a run in the sim is usually followed by an observation question and then a reason or explain question.
- **Mistakes need an explanation.** Spotting errors in worked examples helps a little, and more when an explanation follows, so "spot the mistake" always shows why after it locks.
- **Produce more than you recognise.** Typed recall gave a lasting testing effect where easy multiple choice gave none, so most items ask students to type, build, sketch or order; choice questions are kept for misconception targets and data to interpret.
- **Challenge each misconception more than once, in different contexts**, and pair motion or data graphs with diagrams (IOP Spark advice).
- **Plan the misconceptions first.** In workflow step 4, list 5–8 misconceptions for the unit (IOP Spark, BEST diagnostic questions and physics/biology education research are good sources) and give each at least one item in Learn and one in the Check.

**The bank.** Marks are per part (section 4a-2). "Paper" says how it prints on the worksheet (student answers in blue; blank and key in the same layout).

| Purpose | Type | Student does | Marks | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**The question plan (do this before writing any questions).** For each section, write a short table in the brief or the chapter script: key idea or misconception → type → Learn step or Check. It passes when:
- the Check uses **at least five different types**, with at least one from each of *misconception*, *observe* and *produce*, no more than two *choose one*, and never two of the same type in a row;
- every key idea has at least one typed, ordered, sketched, built or written answer (not only choice questions);
- every listed misconception is targeted at least twice, in different contexts;
- every Learn step that runs the sim asks an *observe* question about that run before any *explain* question;
- one item per section on the main misconception carries the *How sure are you?* add-on.

**Adding a type the sim doesn't have yet.** Build each new type once as a reusable module in the question engine, then reuse it across sims. A type is finished only when it has, in one place each: the engine entry (render, check with one go, lock showing the right answer, saved state, student and model text for print), its label, its sim points, its paper marks, its print layout in student, blank and key modes (and part A or part B), its marking-scheme line with accepted answers, its minutes for the time estimates, and a test that answers it right and wrong. Every type works with touch, mouse and keyboard (buttons as well as dragging), uses seeded shuffles so the sim and paper match, and keeps the no-specificity rule. Force HQ's engine (`force-hq-rebuild/src/js/35-questions.js`) is the reference implementation, and `force-hq-rebuild/docs/Question_Types_Guide.md` has full data shapes for `order`, `pick`, `reason`, `fix`, `freeze`, `spot`, `sketch` and the confidence add-on.

**Observation tools for sims that run over time** (falling, moving, charging, populations changing): a big ⏸ Pause button next to the start button, step buttons ("◀ 0.5 s", "0.5 s ▶") from a stored run history, and a 📸 Snapshot button that saves the paused moment to the Journal's Runs tab with a sentence, chips and a picture of the arena (a small JPEG, newest 20 kept) that expands when tapped; a snapshot nobody can look at is pointless. Freeze-frame and tap-the-moment questions check against these snapshots, so no question forces a re-run. Mask any readout that would hand over a value the student must work out until they have answered.

## 4d. Toddle AI marking: templates, answer keys and marking schemes

**Toddle's AI feedback instructions can't take attachments**, so everything the AI needs is written text, built from the same question data as the worksheets (`aiFeedbackText(sheets, tasks)`), in this order:
1. **How to mark:** student answers are printed in blue and only those are marked; the template's own text, choices and word banks are not student work; the feedback page at the end of each PDF shows the sim's auto-marking (if it disagrees with the visible answer, trust the visible answer and say so); "Steps completed in the sim" ticks are not marked, but mention skipped steps; one go per auto-marked question; blank questions score 0 with a kind note; accept any correct scientific wording; no marks off for spelling unless meaning is unclear.
2. **Feedback style** for the grade: score per sheet with a per-question breakdown, one or two strengths quoting the student, one next step with a hint rather than the answer.
3. **MYP levels:** the strands the task uses with their wording, the counting rule and the level table from `LEVEL_CUTS` (section 4a-2).
4. **Written answer key**, one block per sheet: a header with the sheet title, total marks and strands, then one short line per question with its type, marks, strands and the answer (accepted words for gaps, tolerance for calculations, partial-mark rules), and for draw and explain questions a "Look for:" list plus the model answer. Build tasks list their rubric rows and questions the same way.
5. **Totals:** marks for the task and marks available for each criterion.

The printable files (blank template, answer key, marking scheme) are still made for Tom's records and paper copies:

- **Marking scheme generator** built from the same question data (`markItem`, `schemeForSection`, `schemeForTask`, `markingInstructions`). Marks per type: choose one 1; tick all 2 (1 if one missing or extra); match/table/sort 1 per cell; order 2 (1 if one neighbouring swap); calculate 2 (answer with tolerance range + working/method); fill the gaps 1 per gap with accepted spellings; choose the words 1 per drop-down; answer and reason 2 (answer 1, reason 1); spot the mistake 2 (line 1, correction 1); freeze-frame table 1 per cell, marked for consistency with the student's own snapshot (the key's values are examples); tap the moment 1 per target; sketch the graph 1 per feature check; How sure are you? not marked; draw/explain 1 per checklist point, plus the model answer and "accept any correct scientific wording". Picture-only rows are named "Row N (picture)" in the order the template shows them. Build tasks use their own rubric (section 4e).
- **Each marking scheme page opens with the same marking instructions as the AI feedback text**, so it stands on its own: **mark only what the student added** (printed in blue); the template's own text, choices and word banks are not student work; the answer key is the standard (students needn't match it word for word); use the sim's feedback page to confirm, and if it disagrees with the visible answer, trust the visible answer and say so; students are expected to complete the steps listed under "Steps completed in the sim" before answering, and a blue tick means the sim recorded that step as done (not marked, but mention skipped steps in the feedback); blank questions or an empty template score 0 with a kind note of what is missing; feedback for Grade 6–8: score per sheet with a per-question breakdown, one or two specific strengths quoting the student, one next step with a hint rather than the answer; no marks off for spelling unless meaning is unclear. End with the total marks.
- **Teacher code:** answer keys, marking schemes and the AI feedback text on the teacher page, and the "I'm a teacher" mode, are **locked behind a code that is the first word of the sim's name in capitals** (Sparks Through Time → SPARKS; Force HQ → FORCE; Adapt Lab → ADAPT), case-insensitive. Remember the unlock in localStorage with a "Lock again" button. Locked buttons show 🔒 and scroll to the code box. Blank templates are never locked.

## 4e. Open builders and build tasks

Open interaction matters: every sim has a **Builder's Workshop** (or the sim's equivalent) on the home screen with 2–4 open builders of the unit's key things. Examples: circuit builder (free-form PhET-style board, all parts), atom builder (protons/neutrons/electrons with element card and charge), power grid designer (randomised map), food web builder, ecosystem/island designer, simple machine builder.

- **Free build** plus **template build tasks**. Each task has: id, title, minutes, a goal sentence, a brief, 2–4 **live checks** shown as chips that turn green only when the build really passes (computed from the model, e.g. the circuit solver with what-if tests: "each switch turns off only its own bulb"), a results table (filled from what the student actually did), 1–3 Review-style questions about their own build (each with a model answer), a marking rubric `[[criterion, marks], …]`, a **model build** description and **example results** for the answer key.
- **Builders save and restore state** (`getState()` / `state` option) and expose the SVG for a snapshot.
- **Map builders are randomised from a seed** with varied conditions (coast, river, mountains, windy hills, volcano, forest, farmland; weather such as sun, wind, rain and tides; demand). Students tap squares to read conditions and drag objects onto the map (ghost tile shows green/red for allowed/not allowed, with a reason when refused), use a remove tool (refund if built this round, part-value sale if older, with undo), then run a simulation. Limits keep it honest (e.g. two dams per river, three biomass plants). Tasks can force conditions (calm and cloudy) and set budgets. Draw each square's objects so they never overlap (one object per square, connections drawn between square centres).
- **Students build the connections too** (power lines, pipes, food-web arrows) by dragging square to square, and connections have real consequences: cost per square by terrain, losses with length, capacity limits and upgrades (e.g. high-voltage lines). Unconnected items get a red "!" badge.
- **Simulate a whole year, show an average day.** Run the model across the seasons (e.g. 12 typical days weighted by month), replay the **average day** hour by hour on the map and chart, then summarise in plain words: "There were blackouts on 42 days this year. They happened between 17:00 and 21:00. Why? The sun has set, and the battery is empty. When in the year? Dec, Jan, Feb." Every failure is explained with its cause; nothing goes wrong silently.
- **Charts explain themselves:** a short legend under the chart (line = what's needed, bars = what was made, faded = stored, striped = wasted, red = shortfall) and a "How do I read this chart?" fold-out.
- **Story mode for scaffolding.** Before (or beside) free build, offer 4–5 short chapters that each teach one idea with a prediction first, a few live goals and a short debrief, unlocking in order (teacher mode unlocks all). End with a big open challenge with real trade-offs, e.g. The Big Switch: start from a realistic existing system (a coal and gas grid, already paid for and connected), play in 5-year rounds to 2050 with income, fuel, upkeep, a rising carbon tax, falling clean-tech prices and blackout costs, so switching too fast goes bankrupt and too slow is ruined by the tax. Free build and design tasks also start from the existing fossil system so students have something to work from.
- **Scorecard of positives and consequences.** After each run, show star ratings (reliability, cost, climate) and a card per item listing what's good ✅ and what it costs ⚠️ (materials, whole-life CO₂, land flooded, waste), with totals. Use verified figures with a source line (e.g. IPCC whole-life gCO₂/kWh medians) and avoid oversimplified claims; check every science claim before shipping.
- **Check every build task and story chapter is achievable** with a script before shipping: a Node harness that loads the model source and runs an exhaustive or greedy search over many seeds for the cheapest valid build. **Re-check whenever the model changes** (e.g. moving from one day to a whole year). Then set budgets/targets a little above the hardest map so the challenge is real but always possible.
- **Build sheet** (`buildSheet(builder, task, mode)`), same three modes as worksheets: student (snapshot of the build from the sim, ticked checklist, results table, question answers, all in blue, then a feedback page with the checks passed, no teacher comment box); blank template (dashed "paste a screenshot or draw it" box, empty checklist and results table with the same columns, answer lines); answer key (checklist ticked, marking rubric, model build, example results labelled "students' will differ", model answers). "Make my build sheet" lists anything unfinished with "Go to …" buttons and "Make it anyway".
- Build tasks appear on the teacher page, in step 5 and in the marking schemes like any section.

## 4f. Open research questions, evidence journal, table maker and graph maker (from Tilt Lab)

Use this when a sim has an open "investigation" part where students find their own way to an answer and hand in evidence. Tilt Lab (`tilt-lab/Tilt-Lab-offline.html`) is the reference build. The table and graph code is also kept in `shared/table-graph-maker.js`.

**Research questions.** Open questions in three levels (🌱 Foundation, 🔭 Extension, 🚀 Mastery), about four per level, none with a single route. Each has: the question; "words to use" (chips shown while writing); five or so "ideas a strong answer includes"; one possible answer; and five or six tips.
- **Tips come one at a time** (a button "Ask for a tip"). Each tip names the view or tool to open and what to look for in it, with a "Show this view" button that adds it to the dashboard. Some tips are "think" tips with no view. Tips used is counted and printed on the worksheet. Tom marks on **tips used + quality of the answer**.
- **Answer check (Force HQ style):** the words to use appear above the answer box. After submitting (at least 12 words) the answer locks and shows the key words used (auto-detected with simple word patterns), one possible answer, and a "Did you include…?" list the student ticks. Edit my answer re-opens it.
- **Pop-up instructions** (a short card stepper with Back, Next, Skip and "Do not show this again") explain the flow when the student opens the investigation. A "How it works" button reopens them. Keep one card for each of: choose, explore, tips, evidence, table and graph, answer, worksheet.
- Test the numbers in every "ideas" list and model answer against the sim itself, for the specific planets, places and dates the question names.

**The journal for the chosen question.** A side drawer (the dashboard stays visible) with a sticky status strip (tips used, evidence n of 5, answer state), tips, notes (600 characters), screenshots, tables and graphs, and the answer (900 characters). Saved per student. Picking a question from Guided mode switches to the free dashboard so the student can see the model.

**Evidence.** One shared list per question, **up to 5 pieces in total** across screenshots, tables and graphs, each with a caption of up to 160 characters ("What does this show?"). A 6th tick is blocked with a message. Deleting a table or graph needs a second press.
- **📷 Screenshot button on every view.** Copy the view's canvas onto a new canvas with a title band (view, planet, place, date and the key settings), JPEG at about 0.68 quality, 540 px wide. Draw non-canvas views (a numbers readout) onto a canvas from their text. Keep at most 12 per student, because localStorage is shared and small.

**Table maker (inside the journal drawer, not a pop-up).** The dashboard has to stay visible so students can read a number and type it in. A table has a title, up to 5 headed columns and 12 rows, 24 characters per cell. "Quick headings" chips (Date, Place, Planet, Tilt (°), Daylight (h) and so on) fill the next empty heading. Add and remove row or column buttons; removing a row or column that has writing in it asks for a second press. Students type the numbers themselves; the sim never fills cells in for them.

**Graph maker (a full-height window, ported from the Paper Plane Lab graph tool).** Keep these behaviours:
1. Choose a table, then the x column and the y column.
2. Choose **scatter graph** (numbers along the bottom) or **bar graph** (names along the bottom). Warn if scatter is chosen and the x column has words in it.
3. Write the title ("x vs y") and the axis labels with units in brackets. A "Use my column headings" button fills the labels.
4. Axis numbers: lowest, highest and gap for each axis, plus a **Suggest numbers for me** button (the x-axis starts at 0 when the data does).
5. **Drag each point onto the graph** from a tray of blue chips (scatter), or drag orange name chips into boxes under the graph and then drag the dot on top of each bar to its height (bar). Use pointer events with a ghost chip so mouse, pen and touch all work. A point or bar snaps to the true value when it lands within about 7% of the axis range. Placed points and bars can be dragged again, and a placed chip can be pressed to take it back. A wiggling hint box sits on the empty graph.
6. A **line of best fit** (two draggable round ends) or **curve of best fit** (a third middle dot bends it), and a **join the points** switch.
7. **Green and red rings** show whether each point or bar is in the right place (within about 3.5% of the axis range). A live **checklist** (type chosen, title, x label with units, y label with units, data fits the axes, all data plotted, every point right) sits under the graph.
8. Everything saves on every change. Esc or "Done: back to my journal" closes the window. The canvas is 1000 × 560 and always has a white background, so the same drawing function makes the live graph, the journal thumbnail and the worksheet picture (without rings). No chart library, no internet.
Limits: 6 tables and 6 graphs per student. On a phone the canvas moves above the controls.

**Worksheet for a research question.** Header, name/class/date, the question, a marking strip (tips used, evidence items, key words used, ideas included), notes, the evidence (screenshots two across; tables and graphs full width, each with the student's caption), the answer, and the check-my-answer lists. A teacher also gets a blank copy and an answer key.

**Worksheets never go over two A4 pages (Tom's rule for evidence sheets).** Do not rely on tidy content. Build a fit step into the worksheet viewer:
- Wrap the sheet in an inner element. Before showing it, measure the height in a hidden "print width" state (A4 minus 12 mm margins is about 703 px wide, 1032 px tall per page) and reduce a CSS `zoom` variable in small steps until it fits two pages. The same `zoom` rule applies in `@media print`.
- Pages break only between blocks. So when the sheet holds blocks that cannot split (graphs, screenshots, the marking strip), add up the space lost at each page edge while measuring and fit to about 97% of two pages. Let tables split between rows.
- Cap the content too: text boxes (notes 600, answer 900 characters), 5 pieces of evidence, a limit on rows and questions. Section worksheets use a compact layout (a chip, the prompt in grey, and the student's answer in blue, in two columns), because the full layout runs to five pages.
- In print, hide everything except the worksheet viewer (`body[data-ws] > *:not(.wsv){display:none}`), so a pop-up library or home page underneath never prints.
- Test the worst case with Playwright: maximum text, 5 pieces of evidence including two graphs and three full tables, then `page.pdf` and `pdfinfo` to count pages. The Tilt Lab worst case fits at about 78% zoom.

- **Trace to vector:** convert supplied art to SVG (potrace or vtracer) when parts must stay reshapeable.
- **Open-licence assets:** PhyloPic (organism silhouettes), Kenney, OpenMoji. Check licences and record credits in the backlog/brief. Try npm/GitHub first, since other sites are usually blocked from the sandbox; otherwise ask Tom to attach files.
- Ask for 2–3 reference screenshots before any art pass.

- Headless Playwright on `dist/test.html`: load, check for console errors, then play through guided mode, each mission, explore mode and each builder using the debug hook (`page.evaluate`) where custom interactions are hard to click.
