# Tom's reference pictures (22 Nov 2026)

Use these for every art and layout session (plan sections 4.4 step 5 and 4.7). They show the feel to aim for, not things to copy: never reproduce these games' characters, icons or logos.

## `spore-creature-editor.jpg` (Spore, creature editor): the designer layout
What Tom likes, in his words: "you can see the animal all the time, but I'd like it to be centred. Then menus for different categories are tabbed, that keeps the animal as the centrepiece the entire time and avoids scrolling up and down."
- **The animal is always on screen, big, in the centre**, standing on a small stage, with the biome behind it.
- **Trait groups are tabs** (a row of icon tabs). One group is open at a time, so nothing scrolls.
- Changes show on the animal at once.
- Differences from Spore: centre the animal (Spore puts it on the right); keep the survival bars and the points always in view; options are big, labelled tiles (44 px or more), not tiny icons; words a Grade 6 student can read.

## `alba-island-path.jpg` and `alba-beach-sunset.jpg` (Alba: A Wildlife Adventure): the look of animals and places
- **Flat colour blocks with one shade tone, no outlines.** Shapes are simple and chunky. This is the same rule as plan 4.1, and the target for the rigs.
- **Animals are recognisable from a few strong markings:** the fox is orange with a white chest, white tail tip and black socks and ear tips; the partridge has a bold striped flank and red legs. Each animal gets two or three clear markings, not detail everywhere.
- **Warm, soft light and a limited palette per scene:** golden grass, blue-grey sea, pale sky; the sunset scene tints everything pink and orange. Each biome and time of day should have its own small palette, and night and seasons tint the whole scene.
- **Depth from layers:** a dark foreground tree framing the edge, a middle ground with paths and plants, pale distant hills. Matches the scene rules in 4.1 and 4.5.
- **Small white line-drawn icons for tools** (notebook, map, camera) in a corner, with a red number badge for new things. A good model for the field-station Journal and tool buttons.
- **The field-notebook feel** fits the chosen concept (field station): spotting, noting and identifying wildlife.
