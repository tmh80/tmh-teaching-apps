          About this model
          • The biomes and survival rules are simplified models. The numbers show patterns, not real measurements.
          • Animals face five pressures: body temperature, breathing, predators, food and energy, and water and salt. Plants face water and salt, frost and heat, grazing animals, and light.
          • Each biome has its own temperatures for each season. Water steals body heat far faster than air.
          • Warm-blooded animals make their own heat; fur, feathers, fat, a large body, huddling and shelter keep it in. They need lots of food. Small bodies have a large surface area for their volume, so they lose heat fast.
          • Cold-blooded animals need far less food, but their bodies are only as warm as their surroundings. On land, below their limit they cannot move or feed, so they must hibernate. If no season is warm enough, they cannot survive.
          • Gills only work in water. Air tubes (like an insect's) only supply enough oxygen to small bodies. Breathing through skin needs moist skin and damp air or fresh water.
          • Some conditions are deadly. If an organism is active in conditions it cannot survive, its chance of surviving a year is 0%.
          • In Run, no individual changes during its life. Each generation, the individuals that match the environment best are more likely to survive and have young, so the population changes over time.
          • In the Food web, each population is shown as an index where 100 is its normal level. Eating raises the eater and lowers the food; every population is also limited by space and disease. Producers need nothing but sunlight; consumers die out without food.
          • The energy pyramid uses the 10% rule: about a tenth of the energy at one level passes to the next.
